### Consensus of the three reviews (2026-10-04)

The second reviewer, the third opinion and the supervisor reached one position on the second-round questions. These are supervisor rulings; only the SPA sign-off is the owner's.

- **Holder snapshot.**
  - The mint is read first, at confirmed commitment, with mint authority none; this gives S_A at slot A.
  - Then one one-shot getProgramAccounts call (V1, never the cursor-paginated V2), at confirmed commitment with `withContext` and `minContextSlot` set to A.
  - Filters:
    - legacy SPL: dataSize 165 plus memcmp of the mint at offset 0;
    - Token-2022: memcmp of the mint at offset 0 plus memcmp(165, [2]).
  - Slot B ≥ A, and B within `maxStateSlotLag` of the decision moment.
  - Every account is decoded and checked for its mint; repeated addresses are refused.

  - Owners are aggregated after that. Later per-owner reads only confirm known dev and insider accounts and are never merged into the snapshot.

  - Cards: GATE-1d, GATE-1e, FACTS-1.
- **SPA.**
  - Block length:
    - an expected block length of 3 days, with 5 and 7 as required sensitivities;
    - promotion on the largest p-value;
    - resampling within regimes at registered weights, with no block across an upgrade boundary.
  - The daily unit is P&L over a fixed capital base from the capacity-constrained deployment replay, on one calendar of T = 50 registered days.
  - One joint test covers both benchmarks: each variant gets a t-statistic against zero and one against S0, and one critical value comes from the maximum over all 2K statistics. A variant passes only if both of its statistics exceed it.
  - Studentisation is either Hansen's fixed ω̂ or per-replicate re-studentisation, whichever calibrates better in simulation, frozen before any data.
  - Variants active on fewer than MIN_DAYS days are ineligible, decided from activity counts. An SE floor is fixed in advance.
  - The selected configuration needs its own simultaneous evidence.
  - The owner signs off before the SPA replaces DSR. STATS-1c.
- **DSR while it gates.**
  - Skewness is clamped to min(sample, 0) and kurtosis to max(sample, a registered floor). This only tightens.
  - The paper's worked example (0.9004; 0.9505 at N = 46 and N = 88) is pinned as a unit test.
  - Daily PSR and DSR are diagnostics. Sharpe uncertainty comes from a block bootstrap that recomputes the whole statistic.
  - The third opinion's simulation shows DSR ≥ 0.95 rejecting a true +5% edge in 90–99% of runs on about 50 days. That puts the SPA sign-off on the critical path for pre-funding item 6.
- **Holdout.**
  - Fixed end E = 2026-10-20; one sealed ledger with one endpoint for every registered universe. Entries are cut off before the exit margin, followed by an observation-only tail.
  - The holdout is opened once, and only after a G1 pass. If it is short of the frozen requirement, max(300, n_power, closed form), or of 10 trade days, the result is "not proven".
  - Opening is mandatory once the counts are met.
  - The registry records the attempt index and the α spent, fixes the family size per attempt, and gives G2 its level.
  - Replicates are about 20/α. G2's one-sided false-pass rate is α/2.
  - An attempt is spent once its configuration is registered (see "Closing items").
  - B5 (Melbourne 10-03 01:47 AEST) is read as a decoder boundary, not a market boundary, with a non-gating per-regime line.
  - Days 10-02 to 10-19 (plus a margin day) are holdout evaluation days. They go through the same download, QA, parity and publish path and are never practice days.
- **Closing items (all three agree).**
  - E is the registered UTC entry cutoff. The seal is opened and scored once, only after the observation-only tail has matured.
  - G3 levels follow the direction of safety:
    - The veto-bias composite (holdout lower bound, v₉₅, |Δ|₉₅) uses α/3 for each part, giving at least 95% simultaneous coverage. Wider bounds are stricter there.
    - The consistency checks (candidate rate, mean, fills, reject mix) are "agree" tests. They keep their individual registered levels, because widening them would make "agree" easier. A joint reject-mix test (G-test or chi-square) is added beside the per-reason intervals, and failing either counts as disagree.

## Order and position lifecycle (CORE-1, `packages/core/src/lifecycle`)

- **2026-10-03 · A failed signature read is terminal only at `finalized`.** A failure read at `processed` or `confirmed` may come from a fork that is later dropped, and the original transaction could still land. Acting on it would allow a replacement, which could mean a second buy or an oversell. Waiting for `finalized` costs about 13 s. A success read counts from `confirmed`: booking a fill early is safe, because the books stay open until every other attempt is dead.
- **2026-10-03 · An attempt is dead only when it has failed at `finalized`, or when the confirmed block height has passed its `lastValidBlockHeight`.** A replacement may be signed, and the books closed, only when every other attempt is dead. Each of these events carries the block height it was read at: `sign_replacement` and `reconcile`.
- **2026-10-03 · Balances are the truth.** A fill found after a failed or expired status is booked with a critical alert. If more than one attempt landed, all are booked (`double_fill` alert). An exit that sold more than its quantity is booked, not refused (`oversold` alert).
- **2026-10-03 · A landing after an intent ended is never refused or left to a person.** This replaces an earlier choice made the same day, which the review rejected. It applies to a landing reported for a cancelled, abandoned or filled intent.
  - The landing raises `unbooked_landing` and a `reconcile_orphan` effect. Every tick repeats the effect until the wallet reconciliation books it with the book's `orphan_fill`.
  - A late buy gets its own open position, so exits can protect it at once. A late sell reduces the position it sold from.
  - New entries stay blocked until it is booked; exits are never blocked.
- **2026-10-03 · A refused reconcile is retried.** While an outcome is known but not reconciled, every tick asks for `reconcile_balances` again, so a reconcile refused because another attempt could still land is retried until it succeeds.
- **2026-10-03 · A late landing reported on a dropped fork is cleared only with finalized proof.** The book's `orphan_cleared` event lifts the entry hold, with an alert, only when all of these hold:
  - balances are unchanged at `finalized`;
  - a `finalized` status read for the signature either failed, or, with a history search, found nothing once the *finalized* block height is past the attempt's last valid height.
  The finalized height matters, because a landing just before expiry is not finalized yet. Without this proof the hold stays, which fails safe. Without the event at all, a fork-dropped report would block entries forever and stop unattended paper runs.
- **2026-10-03 · A restart while `signed` never sends the bytes.** The intent becomes `unknown` and waits for expiry, because we cannot prove whether the bytes left before the restart.

## Engine (ENG-1, `packages/core/src/engine`)

- **2026-10-03 · The engine refuses events outside the total order; a live Feed must release in that order.** Events are ordered by (slot, transaction index, instruction index, receipt time, id). An event that is not after the previous one is logged `out_of_order` and refused, and one dated after now is logged `future_event`. Live, facts can arrive late (a slot-99 transaction after a slot-100 one, or after an off-chain status read in its own slot). So the live Feed holds facts behind a slot horizon and releases them in total order. The parity test replays the *recorded release sequence*, refused events included, rather than re-sorting the raw facts. Re-sorting would deliver facts that live dropped, and the decision logs would differ.
- **2026-10-03 · Repeated reconciles are de-duplicated and rate-limited, and no key waits without bound.** The same `reconcile_balances` or `reconcile_orphan` is sent at most once per `minSlotsBetween`, and at most `maxPerWindow` reconciles go out per `windowSlots`. Under that cap:
  - Orphans (unbooked landings, which block every entry) go before balance reads.
  - Each window reserves max(1, ⌊cap/4⌋) places for waiting balance reads, never more than cap − 1, so exits still reconcile while orphan reads keep failing. With a cap of 1, the two kinds alternate window by window.
  - Within a kind, the key served least recently goes first. A key never served counts as served at its first ask, so a key asked continuously waits at most ⌈K / share⌉ + 1 windows.
  - A waiter that was not asked for in the last slot leaves the line but keeps its turn.

  Earlier versions served first come, first served (which starved orphans), then in rounds by send count (which let returning keys starve a persistent one). This version changes which key is sent compared with those, by design: it is a fairness fix, not a refactor.
- **2026-10-03 · Everything a strategy can reach is frozen.** The book, each decision before it is applied, the effects and the log records are all deep-frozen. A strategy cannot change engine state, or a decision after making it, outside the lifecycle. The log cannot drift from its hash. The replay freezes a copy of its input, never the caller's objects.
- **2026-10-03 · Only the replay driver and its fill model hold a `Replay`.** `momentOf` and `pending` reveal whether future events exist, so neither the engine nor a strategy is given one. The engine gets only the `Clock` and the `Feed`.
- **2026-10-03 · Effect runners are synchronous.** Results come back as feed events. A runner that returns a promise is refused, because results it scheduled after an `await` would arrive after the replay ended and be lost.

## Ledger and storage (LEDGER-1, `packages/core/src/ledger`)

- **2026-10-03 · Outcomes live in a second file.** Labels, the experiment registry and gate results go in a scoring file (`@meme-snipe/core/ledger/scoring`), not in the ledger. The ledger's entry point never imports the scoring module, never attaches a file, keeps its connection out of reach (no property holds it) and refuses to open a scoring file. An import guard test starts from every source file outside `ledger/` and `stats/`, follows static, dynamic, `require`, re-export and package-subpath imports transitively, and fails on any path to the scoring store, a ledger internal or `node:sqlite`. Loads it cannot follow also fail it: a computed `import()` or `require()`, `createRequire`, `process.getBuiltinModule`, native bindings, and any string naming sqlite. Gate results count as outcomes: the worker reads them through the scoring reader, never through the engine.
- **2026-10-03 · Amounts are canonical decimal text.** A u64 token amount can pass SQLite's signed 64-bit integer, so every amount column is `TEXT` with a check (digits only, no leading zero, no sign, no decimal point) and is read back as `bigint`. Label net P&L may be negative (`net_lamports`). Return fractions and probabilities stay `REAL`: they are ratios, not money.
- **2026-10-03 · Append-only by trigger.** Every table refuses `UPDATE` and `DELETE`. Every connection the ledger and scoring code open sets `recursive_triggers = ON`, so `INSERT OR REPLACE` fires the delete guard; upserts fire the update guard. A connection opened outside this code without that pragma could still REPLACE a row: the triggers protect against our own code, not against someone with raw file access. State changes are new rows: intent and position events, a release or keep row per reservation, a done row per outbox item, a result row per operator command.
- **2026-10-03 · One writer by OS lock.** (Replaces a pid lock file, which the review showed two processes could both take.) The writer holds `BEGIN EXCLUSIVE` with `locking_mode = EXCLUSIVE` on a sidecar SQLite file `<file>-writer.lock` for its whole life. The kernel drops the lock when the process dies, so there is no stale-lock handling and no race. A second opener in any process is refused after about 1.5 s of retries. Openers racing for a fresh lock can deadlock inside SQLite: each holds a read lock while waiting for the others to drop theirs, and CI caught a run where all six were refused. So each attempt does not wait. On failure it closes, which drops its read lock, then pauses a random 5–45 ms (`crypto.randomInt`, so racers fall out of step) and retries, up to 60 times. One racer wins. The lock lives in `ledger/adapters/lock.ts`, because pausing the thread and drawing randomness are allowed only in `adapters/` folders. A lock file with foreign content fails closed. Reservation atomicity does not rely on this lock: it runs in `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` and is tested with eight processes on separate connections.
- **2026-10-03 · Times come from the caller.** The ledger never reads the clock, so a backtest on a simulated clock writes the same rows as live. This also keeps it clean under ENG-1's purity guard (PR #10). `schema_migrations` records version, name and checksum, with no applied time.
- **2026-10-03 · A file is stamped with its purpose** (`live`, `paper` or `backtest`) when created, and only opens as that purpose, so a backtest can never write into a live ledger.
- **2026-10-03 · Migrations are forward-only and checked.** Versions run 1..n with no gaps, each in its own transaction, recorded with a SHA-256 of kind, version, name and SQL. A file newer than the code, or one whose applied migration differs from the code's (an edit, or the other kind of file), is refused. Schema 1 holds frozen copies of the domain's venue and status lists; a test fails when the domain lists change, so the change ships as migration 2.
- **2026-10-03 · Telegram may only pause, and issuers are roles.** The schema refuses any other command with auth level `telegram` (docs/ARCHITECTURE.md §12), and `issued_by` must be `owner` or `system`, so no Telegram id or email can be stored.
- **2026-10-03 · Node version.** `node:sqlite` is unflagged from 22.13, and the crash tests run child processes with type stripping (unflagged from 22.18). The root `engines` field is `>=22.18`; CI's `node-version: 22` resolves to 22.22.
- **2026-10-03 · Reservation limits fail closed.** A missing or malformed limit (`maxHeld` not a bigint ≥ 0, `maxCount` not a safe integer ≥ 1) throws and writes nothing, never lets a reservation through.
- **2026-10-03 · The ledger stores intent statuses; it does not judge them.** The CORE-1 reducer is the authority on legal sequences. A status-only check in the ledger would refuse legal ones, such as a late landing after `reconciled`. Accepted by review on condition that the replay check is tracked by name: **LEDGER-REPLAY**, under pre-funding gate item 2. LEDGER-REPLAY replays every stored intent and position event through the CORE-1 reducer and fails on any illegal sequence. To be built with BT-1 (it needs the stored events with their full payloads) and added to the task board by the supervisor.
- **2026-10-03 · Guard rules match ENG-1's.** ENG-1's purity guard (PR #10) forbids I/O, clocks, randomness, dynamic `import`/`require` and global escape hatches in core outside `adapters/` folders, which it skips entirely. So the ledger's SQL stays in `ledger/`, where that check runs (an earlier move of it into `ledger/adapters/` was reverted because it took the SQL out of the check). Only the writer lock, which needs `Atomics` and `randomInt`, is in `ledger/adapters/lock.ts`, and it holds no ledger SQL. Since ENG-1 merged, its guard applies that SQL check to every string in `ledger/**`, `adapters/` included, so the ledger keeps no separate SQL test (one was used until then and removed as a duplicate). The ledger's import guard adds the outcome-store rules: no `node:sqlite`, `getBuiltinModule`, `createRequire`, computed import or require, native bindings or sqlite-naming string outside `ledger/` and `stats/`. Checked against the merged guard (base 3f080cf). The guards cover every ordinary way to load a module; deliberate evasion is for code review to catch.

### Ledger replay check (LEDGER-REPLAY, `packages/core/src/ledger/replay`, `pnpm ledger:replay`)

- **2026-10-03 · Book events are stored in the existing `detail` column, with no new table (approved by the supervisor, same day).** The ledger had no book-event log, and `intent_event.seq` and `position_event.seq` are separate sequences. `intent_event.seq` is the global order. Each stored book event writes one intent row per intent the reducer persists, in effect order. The first row carries `detail = {"v":1,"book":<BookEvent>}`, and later rows of the same event carry `null`. A `created` row stands for `propose_entry` (entry) or `trigger_exit` (exit), whose reasons are kept in that step's `position_event.detail` in the same versioned form. An event that persists no intent but changes the book or a position writes one row, with the status unchanged, for the intent it concerns: a landing reported for an ended intent, `orphan_cleared`, or a trigger merged into a running exit. `stepRows` decides the rows, so writers and the replay share one rule. BT-1 and the worker must write this way; `test/ledger/recorder.ts` shows how.
- **2026-10-03 · The stored event is versioned and checked strictly (supervisor's condition).** `encodeBookDetail` (on write) and `decodeBookDetail` (on read), in `ledger/replay/schema.ts`, refuse an unknown or missing version, a missing or extra field at any depth, a wrong type, a non-canonical id or signature, and a negative or non-bigint amount. Events the replay rebuilds from the intent and position tables pass the same check. A new field or event type needs version 2.
- **2026-10-03 · Not stored, not checked:** pause and resume, a restart that changes no intent, and ticks. They only refuse entries, so leaving them out can make the replay more permissive but never changes a replayed state. The default open-position limit is "none" for the same reason. `--max-open-positions` checks a session's limit.
- **2026-10-03 · Read-only means the ledger bytes never change.** The replay opens the file with `openLedgerReader` (SQLite read-only). On a WAL file, SQLite still creates its empty `-wal` and its `-shm` lock index beside the file. These hold no ledger data, and the tests check that the `-wal` stays empty and the file is unchanged byte for byte.
- **2026-10-03 · Stamps.** The file's purpose stamp decides which decision modes it may hold: `backtest` holds only `replay`, `paper` holds `paper` and `shadow`, and `live` holds `live` and `shadow` (shadow runs beside paper and live, quant.md §8). The command refuses files with different stamps in one run.
- **2026-10-03 · Compaction.** The CORE-1 book copies its maps on every event, so a replay of the whole book slowed with the square of the ledger's size (1.9k events a second at 2.5k intents). The replay moves ended intents and closed positions out of the book every 64 events. It brings one back before any event that names it (by intent id, idempotency key, position id or orphan signature). The reducer reads the others only to skip them, so states are unchanged. A test compares the compacted and uncompacted reports on honest and tampered ledgers. The engine pays the same quadratic cost during a backtest, which is a risk for BT-1's throughput target. The replay also reads `fill` and `attempt` once each, grouped by intent, because `fill` has no index on `intent_id` and a read per intent scanned the whole table. Measured on engine-generated backtest ledgers: 22.2k events a second at both 21k and 63k events (7.4k intents), so the cost now grows linearly.
- **2026-10-03 · Whole tables, not only known intents (review of 611a4bb).** A fill, attempt, reservation or reservation event for an intent that does not exist would pass a check that reads rows only by known intent ids. The replay therefore also runs `PRAGMA foreign_key_check` (read-only) and requires the `fill`, `attempt`, `reservation` and `reservation_event` totals to equal the replayed totals. Each table has a test that fails on 611a4bb.
- **2026-10-03 · Named allowance: "held while the outbox row is pending".** When the reducer has released or kept a reservation, the stored reservation may still read held while the `release_reservation` or `keep_reservation` outbox row for that intent is not yet done. The reducer produces none of these, so there is nothing to replay them against. Their stamps and foreign keys are still checked.
- **2026-10-03 · LEDGER-1b: many positions per entry intent (migration 2; supervisor approved the data shape).** A buy that lands after its entry ended gets its own position (`<position>.o<n>`) with the same entry intent, so `position.entry_intent_id` is no longer `UNIQUE`. The position table is rebuilt with the same columns and checks, a plain index on `entry_intent_id`, and its append-only triggers restored. Rows are copied out and back, not renamed, because `ALTER TABLE RENAME` opens SQLite's temp schema on the writer's connection, and the label guard requires that connection to hold only `main`. A migration that rebuilds tables sets `rebuildsTables`. The runner then switches foreign keys off before `BEGIN` (SQLite's 12-step ALTER) and runs `foreign_key_check` before commit, rolling back on any orphan. It switches foreign keys back on whatever happens. The flag is not part of the checksum, so migration 1's checksum is unchanged. Before this fix, a late buy could not be stored and the replay failed it with table `position`. It is now stored and replays.
- **2026-10-03 · LEDGER-1c: reservations carry the account version of their risk snapshot.** `reserveExposure` refuses `stale_snapshot` unless the account version read inside the same BEGIN IMMEDIATE equals the version the decision saw (`accountVersion()`); so two entries decided from one snapshot can never both be stored, even after the first one's reservation ended in a position (risk review of PR #18, item 1). The version is derived, not stored: the sum of the row counts of the append-only tables that change a risk snapshot (reservation, reservation_event, fill, fee, position, position_event, operator_command, command_result), so it only rises and cannot drift; no migration is needed. Intents, intent events and attempts are left out (an entry writes them itself between its decision and its reservation; an unresolved entry is already counted by its reservation). The check fails closed: in a live or paper ledger a reservation, or a book event that creates one, without a version throws; only a backtest ledger, which mirrors decisions already made, may omit it. `recordBookEvent` checks the version once, before the event writes anything. A risk snapshot is built with `withSnapshot(read)` (reader and writer): the version and every inner read share one read transaction, so a write landing meanwhile cannot change what the snapshot saw (risk review of PR #40). Every ledger table is classified: counted (`ACCOUNT_VERSION_TABLES`) or excluded with its reason (`ACCOUNT_VERSION_EXCLUDED`); a test fails on any unclassified table, so a new one (owner latches, deposits and withdrawals) cannot be missed. The data shape is approved under the supervisor ruling in `CLAUDE.md` (the bot's own state, no personal data).
- **2026-10-03 · A mark update cannot change an allowed reservation, so marks are not in the version.** An entry is allowed only with open positions + unresolved entries < maxOpen, and maxOpen ≤ 1 in every approved baseline (tighten-only), so an allowed entry's snapshot has no open position; marks enter the R6 figures only through open positions, so they contribute nothing to its limit. A position that opens after the snapshot advances the version (position, position_event). If the owner ever approves maxOpen > 1, marks must join the version (or the limit be re-read in the reservation) before that baseline is used.

## Configuration and policy (CFG-1, `packages/core/src/config`)

- **2026-10-03 · Code loads limits and tightens them; it never raises them.** A session starts only from a policy that is tighter than or equal to an approved baseline on every field. - **2026-10-03 · Every field has a direction.** `max` (a cap or trigger: may fall only), `min` (a floor: may rise only), `locked` (any change needs a new version), `free` (the label). A new field without a direction does not compile.
- **2026-10-03 · The exit path is locked.** Exit fees, the fee ceiling, min-out and the emergency rung cannot be changed by override, and validation refuses a zero fee or a zero slippage allowance on any rung. A lower number there can stop an exit landing, so it is never "tighter".
- **2026-10-03 · A saved policy is read by a strict reader.** Duplicate keys and `__proto__`, `constructor` and `prototype` keys are refused; every field is checked and covered by the hash; a field supplied through a prototype is not a field. The session copies, then validates, then locks the copy.

- **2026-10-04 · GATE-1d: a partial holder view is judged at its worst case, so splitting a balance cannot hide it.** A holder can spread tokens over any number of accounts, so a largest-accounts list (`getTokenLargestAccounts`, top 20) says nothing about owners or extra accounts it does not show (the smallest-listed-balance bound was wrong: the dev at 50% in 100 small accounts read as 1.4%). The run is done only once a signature at or below the range's first slot was seen; a short or empty page before that stops it as `history-end` with the rest a gap. Rate limits, timeouts, network errors, 5xx and refused queue slots back off at 2, 4 and 8 s (capped at 16 s), 4 attempts per call. A transaction that cannot be read, or has no decodable create, is a one-slot gap. 20 such in a row, a failed signature page, the cap or the halt stop the run, and everything not done becomes one bounded gap. Newest first means a stopped run's gap sits at the old end, which leaves H14's window first.
  - **Gap dating.** Each gap is dated at its last block time, or the next known one, never earlier. If neither is known it is dated at `asOf`, the latest possible and so the safe side. Overlapping gaps merge.
  - **Cost, measured 2026-10-04.** On the public RPC, 1,000 create-authority signatures took 1,254 s and 7.2% failed: about 69,000 a day, about 64,000 creates. `estimateBackfillCredits` gives about **64,100 Helius credits per day of catch-up**, which is 6.4% of the free month. 14 days is about 897,000, more than the 700,000 halt. Published days end on 1 Oct, so from about 15 Oct a full look-back cannot be seeded on the free plan. The run stops at its cap or the halt, and the old end stays a gap. `rpc.creditCap` (accepted by the supervisor): 150,000 (15% of the month, about 2.3 days of catch-up), so live traffic keeps its share. The report carries the estimate, the cap, `fitsCap`, the calls, the credits used, retries, futures dropped and why the run stopped.
  - **Rugs are not seeded (blocker for the dry run, sent to the supervisor 2026-10-04).** Day releases keep trades only for the 5% hash sample, and an RPC trade backfill for every mint costs millions of credits a day. So no `coverage:rugs:*` fact is produced, and under RUG-1's rule the rug half of H14 is not covered for 14 days after any start or restart. Options sent to the supervisor: an on-demand rug backfill per deployer (only the creator's prior mints, a few dozen credits a decision), or saving the index, labeller and coverage state across restarts.
  - **Restarts: saved state plus a downtime fill (supervisor ruling, 2026-10-04).** WORKER-1 saves the deployer index, the rug labeller's state and the coverage facts across restarts (public chain data; the supervisor approved the shape). On restart, `buildSeed({ days: [], fill: { fromSlot, fromMs, close }, rpc, untilSlot, asOf })` backfills only the downtime. It makes no start fact, because the saved coverage continues. Every unfetched slot is a bounded gap on via `seed`. `close` names the saved watch's open gap (`via`, `fromSlot`). The fill range is `fill.fromSlot`..`untilSlot`. A complete fill closes it with a `coverage:creates:resume` up to `untilSlot`, so coverage stays continuous; an incomplete fill closes it with a bounded gap on the same `via` and `fromSlot` (lossy). The close is dated from the restarted watch's actual `coverage:creates:start` fact, which the worker passes as `fill.liveStart`: same slot and in-slot position, at `min(fill.fromMs, liveStart.receivedAt − 1)`, so `createsCoverage`'s order puts it first and that start cannot settle the saved open gap as lossy before it. The feed may place the start below `untilSlot` (after a skipped slot, a socket ahead of the feed tip, or at `released + 1` with no tip), so no slot derived from `untilSlot` is safe. A close without `liveStart` is refused. Tests cover a start at `untilSlot`, one and two slots below it, at `released + 1`, and timed at the downtime's first block (#45 re-reviews). The fill reads from the older of `fill.fromSlot` and `close.fromSlot`, because the close restores the saved gap from `close.fromSlot` and FEED-1's gap starts at the old watch's last log slot, inclusive (#45 re-review: reading more beats refusing). It is empty and complete (no RPC call, a resume) only when `close.fromSlot` is after `untilSlot`, or is null and `fill.fromSlot` is after it (a failover, or a node behind the one that saved the state): the saved coverage then already reaches past the new start. `close.at` is the moment the saved gap was reported. A saved gap reported at or after the restarted start is not settled by that start, so the close then follows the gap by 1 ms; without `close.at` such a gap stays open, which is fail safe (tested). A `liveStart` or `close.at` dated after `asOf` is refused, as `seed` refuses its facts, so no coverage fact is ever dated after the process start. A close that would still land after `asOf` (a `close.at` exactly at it, followed by 1 ms) is not made, so the saved gap stays open (fail safe). Without a fill, the restarted watch's new start settles the open gap as lossy, so H14 is not covered (tested). WORKER-1 must run the fill on every restart, with no RPC if need be: a crash leaves no open gap, and only the fill's gaps keep the downtime from reading as covered. Fill creates go to the restored index through `DeployerIndex.fill(creates, asOf)`, which accepts creates older than live events already seen, with the same as-of and order checks as `seed`, all or nothing, and never moves the index's start. The fill's coverage goes into the engine before the watch's new start (test: restart, fill, coverage continuous). A few minutes of downtime costs a few hundred credits.

## Funding screens (FUND-1, `apps/web/src/funding`)

- **2026-10-03 · What can be withdrawn.** Balance minus the protected SOL reserve (§8 R4, from the worker) minus one transfer fee (5,000 lamports), never below zero. The fee comes off the amount that can be sent so a full send never dips below the reserve. SOL amounts are decimal strings with up to 9 places and all arithmetic is bigint lamports; AUD values use the SOL/AUD price string and round to cents.
- **2026-10-03 · One destination.** The Withdraw form refuses any address that is not exactly the saved wallet, and a malformed one is reported as malformed. A saved-wallet change is a separate request that takes effect 24 hours later; until then Withdraw keeps the old address. Both flows need the passkey step-up first. The app wires a step-up that always refuses (`unavailableStepUp`) until the signer work provides the real passkey check, so no request can be created by accident; the sample-data page uses an approving stub.
- **2026-10-03 · QR code uses `qrcode-generator` 2.0.4; `jsqr` 1.4.0 is test-only.** Supervisor approval under AGENTS.md (package.json). `qrcode-generator`: MIT, no dependencies, no install scripts, exact pin in apps/web `dependencies`; it encodes byte mode at level M and the app draws the matrix as an SVG, so there is no canvas and no network call. Reason: Reed-Solomon and mask rules are easy to get subtly wrong, and a wrong QR on a funding screen is worse than a pinned dependency. (A hand-written encoder was built first and replaced on the supervisor's ruling.) `jsqr`: Apache-2.0, no dependencies, no install scripts, exact pin in `devDependencies`, imported only by `test/qr.test.ts`, never bundled. `test/qr.test.ts` draws the matrix with a quiet zone, decodes it with jsQR and requires the exact address for 32 to 44 character addresses, requires a one-character change to change the decoded value, checks level M and byte mode, and proves the shown address, the QR code and the copy button carry the same value (the button copies from `data-copy`, set from the same variable as the text and the QR). The code is drawn black on white in both themes because scanners read dark on light.
- **2026-10-03 · Requirements for SIGN-1.** (a) After the passkey step-up, the signer path re-checks the destination against the latest saved wallet and the amount against the latest balance and reserve, and the signer re-checks both again; the screen's check is a convenience, not the control. (b) The 24-hour saved-wallet change is shown but not yet stored or enforced: nothing persists a pending change or applies it after 24 hours. Persisting it is a new saved-data shape, so it needs the owner's approval together with SIGN-1.
- **2026-10-03 · A throwing step-up counts as a refusal.** The step-up runs inside a promise chain, so a synchronous throw, a rejection or any answer other than `true` all mean "not approved", and the busy flag is cleared in a `finally`.
- **2026-10-03 · Deposit shows no address before the gate passes.** Kept from WEB-1 (CLAUDE.md, "No deposit before proof"): until the pre-funding gate passes, Deposit shows "Not passed" and no address or QR. The sample-data page passes the gate.

## Transactions and landing (TX-1, `packages/core/src/tx`)

- **2026-10-03 · Wallet-owned accounts and transfer destinations are always static keys.** Venue and infrastructure accounts may load from a lookup table; the wallet, its token accounts, its volume accumulators and the tip account never do. The signer cannot check what a table resolves to, so the policy refuses any of those loaded from one.
- **2026-10-03 · The signer policy recomputes the worst-case SOL out from the decoded bytes.** Base fee, priority fee (from the compute-budget instructions), every transfer out, the curve buy's spend, the larger of the wrap transfers and the PumpSwap buy's `spendable_quote_in` (wrapped SOL already in the wallet's ATA would be spent too; anyone can send some), and each account the swap may create or top up, charged at the rent of the largest such account (170 bytes) at the live rate, never a caller's figure. An account created and closed in the same transaction is not charged. The builder's own itemised figure must never exceed it (review of PR #20).
- **2026-10-03 · A transaction that names any account twice is refused,** by the policy and by DEC-1's `accountKeys`, as the runtime refuses it (AccountLoadedTwice) (review of PR #20).
- **2026-10-03 · A buy quote carries the spend it was computed for; it must equal the request's spend, with at most 3 lamports left unspent by fee rounding** (measured 0 on 40,000 random quotes). A quote made for a smaller spend would size min-out for the wrong trade (review of PR #20).
- **2026-10-03 · The PumpSwap buyback recipient's quote ATA is charged when not read as existing.** All eight exist on chain (slot 452960110) and the IDL does not list them as created, but the program is not open source.
- **2026-10-03 · Rent is charged at SIMD-0437 rates read live.** Token-2022 pump ATAs are sized 170 bytes, SPL accounts 165, volume accumulators 137; an old bonding curve is charged for growth to 151 bytes (pump-sdk 2.0.0; the pinned BUY.md says 115, the larger is used).
- **2026-10-03 · The jitodontfront marker rides on the tip transfer, read-only.** The System program ignores extra accounts; venue programs parse their remaining accounts by position, so the marker is never added to a swap.
- **2026-10-03 · Compute limits come from a calibration table (p99 × 1.1, at least 20 samples).** The provisional table is built from 60 real mainnet swaps per kind with only our instruction set; TEST-2 replaces it with our own simulations. A shape missing from the table refuses the build. A priority fee that rounds to a zero unit price is refused, since Sender requires a price instruction.
- **2026-10-03 · Fee recipients come from the chain-read Global and GlobalConfig, picked by a seeded index.** The same inputs always build the same bytes.
- **2026-10-03 · A broadcast sends only the stored bytes, after checking they carry the attempt's signature and blockhash and are fully signed.** Sender (SWQoS-only, mev-protect) and our RPC get the same bytes with `skipPreflight` and `maxRetries: 0`. A path that answers with another signature is an error. Replacement waits for CORE-1's expiry, reconciliation and dead-attempt proof, checked again before anything is built.
- **2026-10-03 · A status check reads the confirmed slot and block height first, in one `getEpochInfo` answer, and requests the statuses only after it answers; the events carry that height, and a status answer counts only if its `context.slot` is at least that slot** (one RPC URL can be served by several nodes; a lagging node's not-found proves nothing). CORE-1 expires an attempt on "not found after a history search" past lastValidBlockHeight. A height read after (or alongside) the statuses can be newer than the status snapshot, so a transaction landing in between would be expired and replaced: a double trade (review of PR #20).
- **2026-10-03 · The policy charges a created Token-2022 ATA at 170 bytes; it cannot see the mint's extensions.** A mint with size-changing account extensions (transfer fee and the like) makes a larger ATA, so a transaction built outside the builder could be undercounted by about 60k lamports. Only the builder refuses those mints. SIGN-1 may tighten this by taking the mint account (review of PR #20).
- **2026-10-03 · The policy's worst case for a PumpSwap trade includes the buyback recipient's quote ATA as a flat charge,** about 1.5M lamports (one 170-byte account at 5,080 lamports per byte) above the builder's figure when that account exists. SIGN-1's `maxSolOut` needs that headroom (review of PR #20).
- **2026-10-04 · One supported-transaction-shape check for the gates, the backtest and the builders (TX-1b).** `checkShape` (`packages/core/src/tx/shape.ts`) decides mint program, extension set, mayhem and cashback flags, venue, quote mint and the PumpSwap layout (account at least 300 bytes, so our v1 instruction set needs no `extend_account`). `buildTrade` refuses with its reason; the new hard reject H17 runs it on the gate facts right after H5 and rejects with `unsupported-shape` before any entry is proposed. The backtest gets it through the same gates (BT-2 wires them into S0 and the strategies), so its candidate counts never include coins the builders refuse. Reproduced first: on the base the gates admitted 9 shapes the builders refuse (GroupPointer, TokenGroup, GroupMemberPointer, TokenGroupMember and DefaultAccountState=initialized passed H4; a cashback pool, an unread cashback flag, an unread coin creator and a 287-byte pool passed H5), and a pool whose account size was not read passed both the gates and the builders (the builders compared an unread size with 300 and let it through; `checkShape` now refuses it) (`packages/worker/test/shape.test.ts`: every accepted configuration builds and simulates its entry and its exit; every refused one fails H17; TX-1c pins each configuration's exact builder answer and H17 verdict, so a builder loosened on a coin another gate also blocks is still caught, and the exported `curveAccounts` and `poolAccounts` run the same check themselves). H4 keeps its sellability allowlist; H17 is the narrower buildability rule, and the gate tightened, not the builders. The pool fact now carries `accountBytes`, `isCashbackCoin` and `coinCreator`; until a producer (live worker, DATA-1 for the backtest) records them, H16 rejects every candidate, which is the safe failure.

## Live feed and quota scheduler (FEED-1, `packages/worker/src/providers`, `packages/worker/src/scheduler`)

- **2026-10-03 · A live transaction's position in its slot is its rank by first arrival.** No live source gives a transaction's index in its block, so the live Feed sets `txIndex = 2^32 + rank`, ranking each signature in its slot by the arrival order (`seq`) of its first frame. A later arrival never changes an earlier rank, so a late frame cannot reorder what was released. Inside a transaction, `ixIndex` is 0 for the sighting and `1 + outerIx × 2^16 + innerIx` for each decoded event, so events keep execution order and a transaction stays contiguous. Account states sort after every transaction of their slot, then the slot notice, then off-chain facts. The same mapping (`frameEvents`) serves the backtest Feed over recorded frames.
- **2026-10-03 · The live Feed fixes each frame's place at receipt, and the recorder keeps it.** A stream fact sits at its own slot. A fact with no slot (PumpPortal), a Parsed Streams report (delivered at `confirmed`, usually after its slot is released), and an answer to our own lookup that comes after its slot was released are placed off-chain in the open slot, the first one not yet released. Without this, every on-demand decode would arrive late and be refused. Frames record their duplicate flag too, so the backtest Feed reproduces live dedup exactly, even after dedup memory is pruned. Off-chain event ids carry the frame's `seq`, so a copy that returns after its dedup key was forgotten still gets a unique id.
- **2026-10-03 · Late stream facts follow the ENG-1 rule, and the release sequence is recorded.** A stream fact for a slot already released is released at once, marked late, and the engine refuses it as `out_of_order`. Every event handed to the engine produces a `Release` record {index, frameSeq, eventId, late} that the recorder keeps with the frames. The parity replay of recorded live data (`replayRecorded`) feeds exactly that sequence, on a clock that moves as the live one did. Re-sorting the frames (`frameEvents` with `createReplay`) would accept what live refused, so it is kept only for data with no release record (review of PR #21, supervisor ruling). Chain facts older than `keepSlots` behind the release point enter off-chain instead, so ranks and dedup keys can be forgotten without changing any later release.
- **2026-10-03 · Transactions are decoded only by DEC-1, and creates are read from their log lines.** A `logsSubscribe` notification is a sighting of its signature. A fetched transaction is decoded only by DEC-1's `transactionEvents`. The creates stream (`decodeLogs`) also keeps its log lines as their own frame, read by DEC-1's `logEvents`. So every create's creator, mint and slot reach the H14 deployer index at no RPC cost, even when PumpPortal's copy won dedup. Log events sort after a transaction's instruction events, carry a `truncated` flag, and a cut or malformed log becomes a `logs:truncated` or `logs:undecodable` fact, never a guess. Sightings filled by backfill carry no logs, so a watch named for coverage (`coverage: 'creates'`) reports `coverage:creates:start` {fromSlot} when first subscribed and `coverage:creates:gap` {fromSlot, toSlot, reason} for every range it may have missed: each reconnect when it reads logs, otherwise a backfill that failed or filled a whole page; `toSlot` is null when the watch is unwatched, halted or refused. A gap starts at the watch's own last log-notification slot, inclusive (slot notices run ahead of logs, so creates of that slot may still have been in flight). The gap is reported open (`toSlot` null) at the moment of the drop, so decisions made during an outage, or a stream that never recovers, see the range as uncovered. When it closes it is settled by a bounded gap with the same `fromSlot`, or by `coverage:creates:resume` when backfill restored it in full. It ends at the first slot this stream sees live after the resubscribe, never at another provider's tip, and only once that connection's backfill has finished. Every drop and open moves a connection epoch, so a backfill from a lost connection can never close a newer gap. After `resetBudget` the stream accepts watches again, and a new start fact marks where coverage resumes (review of PR #21). GATE-1's deployer index marks those ranges uncovered and H14/H16 reject deployers whose history touches them, never counting fewer mints (supervisor, 2026-10-03). Decoding every create is not affordable: about 50k creates a day is about 1.5M `getTransaction` calls a month, 150% of Helius Free's credits or twice Alchemy Free's 30M CU. So the worker fetches only where it decides to: migrations (needed for `CompletePumpAmmMigrationEvent`), shortlisted tokens and the open position. Every other create stays a sighting.
- **2026-10-03 · Rate buckets are sliding windows.** Provider limits are windows: Jupiter documents a 60 s sliding window, and Helius counts requests per second. A refill-rate token bucket either bursts past such a window or wastes part of it. Each provider bucket grants at most `limit` per window, keeps `floors[c]` grants free for higher classes, and has optional caps by class or lane. RugCheck runs at one request per 4.5 s, the only rate verified never to hit a 429.
- **2026-10-03 · At 70% of the monthly credits only P0 requests run, and the open position stays watched.** New requests of P1–P3 are refused, P2–P3 stream watches are dropped, and new P1–P3 watches are refused. Existing P0–P1 watches stay open. The card says all non-exit traffic halts, but the open position's stream is what triggers its exit. Closing it would leave an exit with no fresh state, so keeping it is the safe reading of "exits are never starved". Stream bytes are still metered.
- **2026-10-03 · The month's credit use is loaded at start and reported on every spend.** A restart must never reset the 70% halt. The scheduler takes `creditsUsed` and calls `onSpend` with each new total. Persisting it is WORKER-1's job.
- **2026-10-03 · A reconnect holds the release point below the missed slots for up to 3 s.** While a stream's backfill runs (`getSignaturesForAddress` since the last seen signature for each log watch, `getAccountInfo` for each account watch; always at P0 for P0–P1 watches, because they serve the open position and the 70% halt must not starve them), the release point stays below the first slot it may have missed. Backfilled facts therefore arrive on time and are marked `backfilled`. After 3 s the hold ends, and later backfill answers follow the late rule. With no tip progress for 2 s, everything held is released (a stale feed never blocks the engine), and a `feed:status:<provider>` fact reports each drop, refusal and recovery.
- **2026-10-03 · The worker imports core by relative path.** That adds no dependency and no lockfile change. Switching to a `workspace:*` link is the supervisor's call.

## Backtest and paper fills (BT-1, `packages/backtest`, `packages/core/src/fills`)

- **2026-10-03 · Our trades change the pool every later trader sees.** The dataset gives each real PumpSwap swap's pre-trade reserves. Without our trades, the pool after a swap is that swap replayed on its own pre-trade state with the exact CORE-2 math. Once our trade lands, the pool carries a delta; each later real swap is replayed on (pre-trade + delta) with its own input (exact base, exact quote in, or base sold), and the delta becomes what remains after it. Pre-trade reserves in each event resync the real state, so liquidity and boost changes between swaps are carried without modelling them. A real swap that would fail on the shifted pool is skipped and counted.
- **2026-10-03 · Fee rates are the ones each trade paid.** Every swap event carries its LP, protocol and creator rates, so replays and our fills charge exactly those (our fill uses the latest real swap's rates on that pool, the same market-cap tier to within one trade). Replaying all 353 CORE-2 golden swaps with their own rates reproduces their fees and amounts exactly (test).
- **2026-10-03 · Our order lands after every real transaction of its landing slot** (and after the slot's block row), as a driver hook the engine never sees. Status and balance reports come back as world events after it.
- **2026-10-03 · Landing, failures and expiry.** Each attempt draws its latency and fate from the world seed (two draws, fixed order): lands and executes (p_land, §11 defaults 0.66 PumpSwap, 0.49 curve), lands failed (pays base and priority fee, no tip), or never reaches a block (`dropPpm`, default 0: costs nothing, expires after its last valid block height). A landing that executes still fails if the output after the scenario's extra slippage is below min-out. Rebroadcasts of the same bytes never draw again. Balances are reconciled only once no attempt can still land, so the lifecycle never sees a refused reconcile.
- **2026-10-03 · Scenarios** live in `config/fills.ts` (versioned like the policy). Conservative: p90 latency, slippage shortfall ×1.5, close-based take-profit, no rent recovery. Latency values are placeholders until the dry run measures ours (§21).
- **2026-10-03 · mints.csv is never read by the run.** It holds whole-life facts (graduation time, tape end, censoring). Lifecycle facts reach the engine only as the events of the day they happen; the discovery of a graduation arrives after a per-mint lag drawn from its own seed.
- **2026-10-03 · SOL/USD for sizing and the report** comes from Coinbase Exchange hourly candles (public, no key), fixed series, closed candles only, each usable from its close plus one bar (`packages/backtest/data/sol-usd-1h.csv`, refetchable with `scripts/fetch-sol-usd.ts`). If DATA-1 ships its own off-chain series, the reader takes it (same tag rules).
- **2026-10-03 · Ticks and slot events.** While an intent is in flight, every block delivers a `slot` market event and a lifecycle `tick`; otherwise a heartbeat slot event every 150 blocks (about a minute), so a time stop fires within a minute of its time (pool swaps also wake the strategy). Per-block events while merely holding would add millions of log records over 30 days. Live records its own ticks, which the parity replay feeds back.
- **2026-10-03 · S0 for BT-1** uses universe U2's membership filters (canonical PumpSwap pool, SOL quote, not mayhem) and the policy's time stop; GATE-1's hard rejects and EXIT-1's exits are added to S0 and the strategies in BT-2. The backtest has no open-position cap (§14) but keeps CORE-1's rule of one entry in flight at a time. Fees of entry attempts that never filled are kept as separate costs in the results, not inside a trade.
- **2026-10-03 · Holdout mode returns two things only:** the sealed ledger file's sha256 and, per universe, candidates, entries and entry days (STATS-1's `HoldoutCounts` shape). The file is made read-only; a holdout file that already exists is refused.
- **2026-10-03 · RUN-1 worker contract (§12.4).** RUN-1 defines it because WORKER-1 has not started; WORKER-1 implements the same contract. Health and drills on a loopback HTTP port (no inbound port opens), the feed-drop drill guarded by a per-boot token file; the journal is a JSONL file with a gapless `seq` so completeness is checkable without the ledger schema.
- **2026-10-03 · Restart drills wait for a trade.** Each restart drill opens a window at its pre-set time and kills at the first open trade or unresolved intent in it, or at the window's end (recorded "not mid-trade"). Six are planned so that at least three land mid-trade in a quiet market. Uptime credits each ready sample up to two sample intervals, so any gap (a runner crash, a fallback handover) counts as down.
- **2026-10-03 · Qualifying run starts by a merged file.** The host starts the qualifying run when the deployed release holds `packages/runner/qualifying-run.json`, once per name, and resumes an unfinished run after a reboot. Nobody logs in to the host, and the start is a reviewed change.
- **2026-10-03 · Fallback artifacts.** Each job uploads `dryrun-state` (state, evidence, logs; 90 days) and `dryrun-recorded-<n>` only after the secret scan passed; recorded files move out of the carried state so each job's artifact holds only its own data. Jobs run the worker for 5 h 35 min to leave room for setup, scan and upload inside GitHub's 6 h limit.

## Dry-run simulation (TEST-2, `packages/worker/src/dryrun`)

- **2026-10-03 · The dry run has no send path, proved twice.** Its RPC client has a frozen list of three methods (`getMultipleAccounts`, `getTokenLargestAccounts`, `simulateTransaction`), checked before the scheduler or the network is touched. A test walks the static import graph from `dryrun/index.ts` and fails if it reaches the TX-1 landing client, its adapters or a barrel that re-exports them, names a send method, or loads code dynamically. The builders and the signer policy are imported file by file for that reason, never through `core/src/tx/index.ts`.
- **2026-10-03 · Stand-ins (supervisor ruling): a funded plain wallet for buys, a current holder for sells.** Buy stand-ins are a configured list of public addresses; each must be System-owned with no data and hold the build's worst-case SOL out plus the rent-exempt minimum. Sell stand-ins come from `getTokenLargestAccounts`: the token account must be the owner's ATA, initialized (not frozen), hold at least the position, and its owner must be a plain wallet that can pay the fees. None qualifies: "not simulable", a failure. The record names the stand-in.
- **2026-10-03 · The stand-in build is checked against the bot-wallet build on every trade, not only in tests.** Same programs, same instruction data (amounts, limits, fees, tip), same metas, after replacing the seven wallet-derived accounts (wallet, base ATA, wSOL ATA, both volume accumulators and their wSOL ATAs). Any other difference, including a venue account replaced by a stand-in-derived key, records "structure-mismatch".
- **2026-10-03 · One allowed difference: a sell's close of the base account is left out when the holder has more tokens than the position.** A token account with a balance cannot be closed, and `simulateTransaction` has no state overrides, so keeping the close would fail every such sell for a reason that cannot happen to the bot wallet. The close is removed after building (same compute limit and price as the real shape) and the record says `closeOmitted`; the report counts them. A holder with exactly the position keeps the close.
- **2026-10-03 · Amounts are measured from balances; rent is the rent actually paid (review of PR #28).**
  - **Buys:** the change in the stand-in's base-token balance, against the quote's tokens.
  - **Sells:** the change in the stand-in's lamports, wSOL account and base account, plus the base fee, priority fee and tip the build declares, plus the rent the transaction actually paid. Rent is counted as in SIM-1 (review of PR #59): a new account counts at most its rent-exempt minimum. The creator vault counts only what it gained beyond the creator fees in the stand-in's TradeEvents, and no top-up when the log was cut. Growth is measured against the length read before. The base-token balance must fall by exactly the position.
  - **Rent paid:** every account the build writes is read before and read back after, and the rent is taken from that state: a new account's lamports (for a wrapped-SOL account, its rent reserve), added bytes × the rent rate for a grown account, and the pump creator vault's top-up to rent-exempt only, since the vault also receives fees. Rent paid above what the build declares is an "amount-check" failure: the builder under-counts. Both figures are recorded.
  - **Where balances come from:** the stand-in's own balances come from the node's `preBalances`, `postBalances` and token balances inside the same simulation, when the provider returns them (Agave 4.3.0 does, checked 2026-10-03). These are atomic, so a transfer landing between our read and the simulation cannot move the amount. Otherwise they come from one `getMultipleAccounts` read at `processed`. The post balances must equal the accounts read back, or the trade is "malformed".
  - **Fee read-back:** the read-back fee payer already has the base and priority fee taken out (docs/evidence/DRYRUN_SMOKE.md).
  - **Why not venue events:** pump's TradeEvent has no field verified to equal the wallet's net SOL on sells. Its buyback fee is non-zero in the golden sells, and that fee's share is unverified.
- **2026-10-04 · Mechanics diagnostics in the report (supervisor ruling, 90fac89).** The report also counts:
  - final-exit simulations (sells that close the position's token account);
  - those that ran with the real close instruction;
  - those that completed both the sell and the close;
  - omitted closes, each with its reason ("the holder holds X, the position is Y").

  They are labelled "mechanics diagnostics; not a landing or rent-recovery probability". The rent model stays the modelled close.
- **2026-10-03 · The error is |simulated − quoted| / quoted in units of 0.0001 percentage points, rounded up.** The report decides the gate in integers: success share ≥ 95% (every outcome but "simulated" is a failure, "not simulable" and scheduler refusals included), median ≤ 0.5 points and every trade ≤ 2 points, over the successful trades.
- **2026-10-03 · Follow-up: the creator vault's top-up ignores the creator fee paid into it in the same transaction** (review of PR #28). The vault counts as topped up by `rentExempt(0) − its balance before`. If the trade's creator fee lands first and already covers rent, the wallet pays less than that, or nothing, so `rentPaid` can overstate the rent and the measured sell proceeds by up to one rent-exempt minimum (about 0.00065 SOL). This only happens when the vault is missing or below rent. To fix it, take the vault's top-up from the program's order of transfers (or from inner instructions) once that order is verified.
- **2026-10-03 · Dry-run calls run at P2 or P3 on the Helius scheduler, never P0 or P1** (both refused with a RangeError before any request; review of PR #28). The worker uses P2. A scheduler refusal is recorded as a failed trade, never retried silently. Each call is a standard RPC call (1 credit).
- **2026-10-03 · Holders are tried largest first, then by token-account address,** so equal balances never depend on the provider's order. Each record carries `quoteAgeSlots` (read slot − quote slot); the report gives its median.
- **2026-10-03 · A bot-wallet build that the signer policy rejects is not simulated** and counts as a failure ("policy-violation"): live, it would never be signed.
- **2026-10-03 · The live smoke test is a script, opt-in like FEED-1's probe** (`packages/worker/scripts/dryrun-smoke.ts`, `ZEROED_LIVE_PROBE=1` and a Helius key, or `ZEROED_SMOKE_PUBLIC=1` for the keyless public RPC). The worker guard test forbids real network adapters in `test/`, so it is outside the test glob and never runs in CI. Runs are recorded in `docs/evidence/DRYRUN_SMOKE.md`.

## Exit engine (EXIT-1, `packages/core/src/exits`)

- **2026-10-03 · One pure step per position update.** `decideExit(settings, plan, holding, tracker, observation)` returns the next tracker and one decision: `hold`, `exit` (quantity, partial or full, retry or not, starting rung, attempt budget, and the liquidation quote it starts from) or `merge` (reasons added to the exit owner in flight). `exitBookEvents` turns it into CORE-1 book events, so the book's single exit owner is what prevents an oversell. The module imports nothing from `risk/` and takes no risk input: no cutoff, pause or kill latch can block an exit (§8, §18; a test checks the imports and a paused book).
- **2026-10-03 · Trigger value.** Every value trigger reads the CORE-2 sell quote of the whole held quantity on the current pool or curve, fees deducted. Candles feed only the ATR. A market state older than `gates.maxQuoteAgeMs` is not a quote; anything stamped after now is dropped and reported. Prices compare by cross-multiplying (`PRICE_SCALE`, a unit in `config/scale.ts`), so stop levels are exact.
- **2026-10-03 · Readings of §9 and risk.md where the text leaves room.** (a) "Not ≥ +0.5R by T_flat" means reached at any update up to and including T_flat; reached later does not count. (b) The k-th partial needs k × the first threshold (1.5R or +100%, whichever first); from 2 × q_min that allows a second partial at 3R, and at q_min only one. (c) The partial sells `ceil(quantity × partialMinShareBps)`; if that is the whole holding it is a full exit. (d) Runner rules (break-even after costs, chandelier trail at peak − 3 × ATR(14, 1-minute)) start after the first partial, as §9 says; risk.md's "after +1R move to break-even" is not added (it would need a new threshold; §9 is the spec). The trail level never moves down. (e) A full-exit trigger and a take-profit in the same update give one full exit carrying every reason (§18).
- **2026-10-03 · Ladder.** Min-out is measured from the trigger value, fixed for the exit owner. A rung whose min-out is above the fresh quote is skipped without spending an attempt (it would only burn its fee on chain); when no rung can be met the exit is blocked now. Fees are capped at `maxFeePerAttempt`. **The budget is per position, not per exit owner** (review of PR #33): the count is the signed attempts of the position's exit intents in the book (`exitAttemptsOf`, passed in as `Holding.exitAttempts`), so a restart with a fresh tracker cannot hand out a new ladder (review round 2); an owner that ends unfilled leaves the position open, and the next owner gets only the attempts that remain, starting above the highest rung already tried (`noteAttempt` keeps that rung; after a restart the attempt count is the floor, since every attempt went at least one rung up). Blocked retries used are `max(tracker, attempts − maxAttempts)`, so they are bounded by the book too (escalation never goes down, also within one owner after a skip). With the budget used, a trigger books the exit blocked at once with a critical alert; only the bounded blocked retry follows, never a new ladder.
- **2026-10-03 · Blocked exits.** An exit with no executable quote is booked `exit_blocked` in the same step (critical alert, nothing sold, never a fabricated fill). The position stays watched; a retry is a new exit owner at the last rung with one attempt, no sooner than `exits.blockedRetryMs` after the block, at most `exits.blockedRetryAttempts` times, and only when the fresh quote pays more than that attempt costs (base fee + the last rung's priority fee capped at `maxFeePerAttempt` + tip). Bounded so a dead pool cannot drain the fee reserve. New policy fields (schema 2): `partialAtGainBps`, `atrPeriod`, `atrBarMs`, `blockedRetryMs`, `blockedRetryAttempts`, all locked against overrides.
- **2026-10-03 · Risk noted for RISK-1: attempts and SOL per position.** At most `ladder.maxAttempts` (5) ladder attempts plus `blockedRetryAttempts` (5) single retries, so **10 attempts per position**. Each costs at most base 5,000 + priority 500,000 (the cap) + tip 5,000 = 510,000 lamports (a failed one 505,000: the tip reverts), so **≤ 5,100,000 lamports (0.0051 SOL) per position** at the trial settings. R4's reserve covers `reserve.exitAttempts` (5) attempts; the retries can add up to 2,550,000 lamports (0.00255 SOL) beyond it. Mitigation: each retry needs a quote above its cost, and every bound is in the locked policy.
- **2026-10-03 · Partials and owners.** Sales are counted as one partial per exit owner (`exitSeq`), so a partial filled in two steps is one partial. **Note for BT-2:** a stop that fires while a partial is in flight only adds its reason to that owner (one exit owner); the stop fires again on the first update after the partial resolves and then sells the rest. Between those two updates the runner is unprotected by a new intent, so the backtest's latency for that second exit is the time to the next update plus the normal landing draw.
- **2026-10-03 · Restart: what the tracker holds and WORKER-1 must restore.** The attempt budget and the retries are rebuilt from the book. The rest of `ExitTracker` resets with a fresh tracker: `peak` and `trail` (a reset trail is looser, so the stop moves down), `flatMet` (the time-flat stop may fire when it would not have), `partials`/`partialSeq` (runner rules switch off), `quoteFailures` and `blockedAtMs` (the retry wait restarts, which only delays retries). **WORKER-1 restart-drill acceptance:** restore `partials` from the book (the position's `exitSeq` and `sold`), and `peak`, `trail` and `flatMet` by saving the tracker with each step or replaying the position's updates from the as-of store; a drill that kills the worker with a runner open must show the same trail level after the restart. **The universe is stored with the position at entry** (CFG-2): it is a fact from entry time, and a restored position rebuilds its `EntryPlan` from it. There is no default, because `exitsFor` throws without one. The drill must show a restored U1 position using U1's exits.
- **2026-10-03 · Validation.** `blockedRetryAttempts` and `atrPeriod` are whole numbers and not negative by the general range check of every policy number; `atrPeriod ≥ 1` is checked explicitly. Tests cover −1, 1.5 and 0.
- **2026-10-03 · EXIT-1b: an exit's quote is for the quantity it sells.** Triggers read the whole holding's executable value; the `exit` decision's `value` is the liquidation quote of its own `quantity`, so a partial's min-out (`planAttempt(ladder, n, decision.value, freshQuoteOfThatQuantity)`) and the fill describe the same sale. Before, a half-position partial carried the whole quote (9,878,763 vs 4,939,689 lamports) and every rung's min-out was above the half's real proceeds: blocked in an unchanged pool.
- **2026-10-03 · EXIT-1b: a blocked retry must pay for itself after the worst slippage.** The retry goes at the last rung, so the least it may receive is the quote less that rung's slippage (25%); that, not the quote, must exceed the attempt's cost, or the position waits.
- **2026-10-03 · EXIT-1b: a full exit that fires during a partial is taken the moment the partial resolves.** Its reasons are merged into the in-flight owner (which sells only its share) and remembered in the tracker (`pendingFull`); the first step on the open position with a fresh quote exits the rest with those reasons, on the normal ladder. A missing or stale quote at the fill is a timing artefact, so that step holds and keeps the reasons (review of be82457: booking it blocked cost the 60 s retry wait, the ladder and a retry, and raised a false blocked alert); a trigger that fires without a quote (time, deployer, route, quote failures) still exits at once, and a real no-quote condition reaches `exit_blocked` through the quote-failure path as for any full trigger. The worker calls `decideExit` on every change to the position (fills included), not only on new market states, so "next step" is the fill itself.
- **2026-10-03 · EXIT-1b: sell before reclaiming rent; a bounded sell-only recovery path.** The exit decision says whether the order may also close the token account (`closeAccount`): only a full exit of a clean account (balance equal to our quantity) with no failed close before. Otherwise it sells only, and the rent stays locked until a later close succeeds. Dust or tokens someone sent us never block the sale: our quantity is sold and the account left open. Once a sell-and-close is resolved failed at the close (`Holding.closeFailed`, classified by TX-1b), later exits sell only, within the same per-position ladder and blocked retries, so the recovery path is bounded. Worst-case reservations keep counting the full rent.

## Live screens on the phone (APP-2, `apps/web/src/api/server.ts`, `poll.ts`, `connection.ts`)
- **2026-10-03 · Address shape.** Only `https://<machine>.<tailnet>.ts.net` (optional port) is accepted: the worker stays on loopback and OPS publishes it with `tailscale serve`, which gives that name a publicly trusted certificate. A login, path, query or fragment is refused, because each could carry a secret or point the app elsewhere. The saved value is checked again on every read. Stored on the phone only: the address and the time the server last answered (app-local, not personal data, never sent anywhere).
- **2026-10-03 · Read-only.** The client issues GET requests with no credentials (tested against a contract mock server). Pause, close and signing controls stay disabled until a later task adds an authenticated owner command path; that needs its own review.
- **2026-10-03 · Native HTTP on Android.** Requests go through `CapacitorHttp`, as the report file already does, so the worker needs no CORS headers and the tailnet name resolves through Tailscale's VPN like any other app request. Risk: Android's Private DNS can bypass MagicDNS on some phones; mitigation: the app shows "Offline", and Tailscale's own app reports the DNS problem.
- **2026-10-03 · Polling, not streaming.** The contract has GET endpoints only. Paper and live poll every 10 s, then 20, 40 … up to 5 min after failures (±10% jitter), and stop entirely while the app is in the background. Capacitor keeps WebView timers running in the background (`KeepRunning` defaults to true), so the document `pause`/`resume` events stop and restart polling as well as `visibilitychange`. A backtest loads once and retries only after a failure. Requests time out after 8 s.
- **2026-10-03 · Offline.** A request that gets no HTTP answer counts as offline. The app then shows "Offline · last update <Melbourne time>", and paper/live data already on screen stays, marked stale; its "updated X ago" note re-reads the clock every 30 s. A section with no data shows "Offline" (with the last update when known) or "No server", never its empty state, since "No open trade" or "No trades" would be a claim the app cannot make (review of #43).
- **2026-10-03 · Last update means good data.** `lastOk` moves only when a response passes `checkEnvelope`. An HTTP error, a non-JSON body or data that fails the checks shows "Server error" with the last update unchanged, plus a separate "Last answer" time (review of #43).
- **2026-10-03 · Server error per endpoint (APP-2b).** The connection keeps the last answer per endpoint (path; one key per mode for the calendar, whatever the month). It shows "Server error" while any endpoint's last answer was bad, so one broken endpoint no longer flips the card between Online and Server error on every poll. A bad answer stops counting after 10 minutes without another answer from that endpoint (longer than the 5-minute backoff plus the 8 s timeout), so an endpoint the app stopped polling, after a mode switch, cannot hold the error forever.
- **2026-10-03 · Serve, not Funnel (note for OPS).** A Tailscale Funnel URL has the same `*.ts.net` shape as a `tailscale serve` one, so the app cannot tell them apart. Funnel would publish the read-only API to the internet; keeping it on serve (tailnet only) is checked in OPS-1e's acceptance, not in the app.

## Risk policy (RISK-1, `packages/core/src/risk`)

- **2026-10-03 · Pure functions over injected inputs.** `evaluateEntry` and `evaluateExit` read the time only from the injected clock (`receivedAt`, structurally ENG-1's `Clock`) and every limit from the locked session policy. Loss figures are derived from the ledger's account history, not passed in, so the day and week boundaries are applied in one place.
- **2026-10-03 · Melbourne day and week by rule, not Intl.** A two-zone table (AEST +10, AEDT +11; first Sunday in October 02:00 AEST to first Sunday in April 03:00 AEDT, the Victorian rule since 2008) in integer milliseconds. Instants before 2008 are refused. The test cross-checks every change from 2008 to 2040 against the tz database. Three non-money constants (`MS_PER_MINUTE`, `EPOCH_YEAR`, `RULE_FROM_YEAR`) are on the money-literal allow-list by exact name and value; supervisor OK requested on PR.
- **2026-10-03 · Equity counts marked losses, never unrealized gains.** E = realized equity + Σ min(0, mark − cost) for open positions, net of deposits and withdrawals; the high-water mark follows realized equity, and a flow moves both. An unknown or stale mark (older than `gates.maxQuoteAgeMs`) refuses entries and counts as a total loss in the figures. A deposit never hides a loss: L_day and L_week add the flows since the boundary back.
- **2026-10-03 · C is the worst case.** Maximum costs of one trade = CORE-2's fixed costs + the token-account rent even when the exit closes it (a blocked exit keeps it locked) + every attempt the exit path can make at the fee cap (EXIT-1: one ladder of `maxAttempts` per position, then `blockedRetryAttempts` single-attempt retries), replacing the modelled exit. R4's live reserve covers `reserve.exitAttempts + blockedRetryAttempts` attempts at the fee cap. R6 reserves q + C; R7 adds C to today's loss. R5 (planned risk, 1R) uses the expected costs instead: q × (s + cost-gate ceiling) + F ≤ 2.75% of B. Transient rent (an account opened and closed inside one transaction, such as WSOL) is not in C: the transaction is atomic, so it always comes back; it needs cash, so the R4 cash cap subtracts it.
- **2026-10-03 · R15 compares like with like.** `ClosedTrade.notional` is the entry's notional q as decided (`EntryAllowed.notional`), costs excluded, and a new q may not exceed the last losing q. (`OpenPosition.notional` is the cost basis, entry costs included, because it measures what the position can still lose.) An entry in a mint with an open position or an unresolved entry is refused.
- **2026-10-03 · The full-loss reservation limit is min(E − 0.7·HWM, 0.2·E_week_start − L_week) minus what open positions can still lose** (mark or cost, whichever is lower, plus their exit ladder). Held reservations are inside the limit. The limits come from an account snapshot the store cannot recompute, so every request carries the snapshot's account version and the store refuses it (`stale_snapshot`) if the account changed since; every account change (reservation, release, fill, position, closed trade, flow) advances the version. So two entries decided from one snapshot can never both be stored, even after the first one's reservation was released into a position (risk review of PR #18, item 1). LEDGER-1 must add the version check to `reserveExposure`, inside the same BEGIN IMMEDIATE.
- **2026-10-03 · Trips are latched by the caller.** The weekly trigger and the kill switch return `trips` from both `evaluateEntry` and `evaluateExit` (a marked dip seen while managing an exit is latched too); the caller stores the time. A weekly trip stays until its week is over and the owner has reviewed it strictly after the trip; a kill trip stays until the owner re-arms strictly after the trip, and the re-arm restarts the high-water mark at the equity of that moment. The R8 5-in-20 review counts only trades closed after the last review. The latch fields (`killRearmedAtMs`, `weeklyReviewedAtMs`, `lossReviewedAtMs`, `sizeStepUpApproved`) record owner actions: LEDGER-1 and the API must let only an owner command write them.
- **2026-10-03 · Sizes stay at q_min until the owner approves a step-up** (`sizeStepUpApproved`, the G5 decision); any 10% drawdown from the high-water mark returns to q_min. q_min in lamports is rounded up (the minimum spend), so a size cap of exactly q_min (R2, R15) admits that minimum spend and nothing between it and the cap; loss caps (R4, R5, R6, R12) admit nothing extra, and the reservation is checked exactly in lamports. The reported notional of a larger trade is its spend valued rounded down.
- **2026-10-03 · Invalid input refuses, never defaults.** History, entries or latch times in the future, a mark without a time or below zero, negative held reservations, equity or week-start equity at or below zero, and a SOL price of zero all refuse an entry (R1). A bad clock throws from an entry (the Melbourne rules refuse the instant) and never from an exit.
- **2026-10-03 · Mutation testing is the evidence that each control is tested** (risk review of PR #18). `research/risk/mutate.py` makes one-line mutants of the risk source and runs the risk tests against each. Every surviving mutant is either killed by a test or listed in the PR as equivalent, with the reason.
- **2026-10-03 · The real store checks the version (LEDGER-1c, PR #40).** `Ledger.reserveExposure` takes `accountVersion` and refuses `stale_snapshot` in the same BEGIN IMMEDIATE; the version is derived from the append-only account tables (see "Ledger and storage"). Marks are not part of it: an allowed entry has no open position while maxOpen ≤ 1, so a mark cannot change its limit; a baseline with maxOpen > 1 must add marks to the version first.
- **2026-10-03 · Exits are never blocked.** `evaluateExit` always allows, reports the tripped entry controls for the log, and never throws, whatever the inputs.
- **2026-10-03 · Freshness without new policy fields.** SOL price, SOL balance, marks and the pool quote must all be younger than `gates.maxQuoteAgeMs` (2 s). Regime freshness is GATE-1's job; risk takes on, off or unknown.
- **2026-10-03 · Deposits and withdrawals are time-weighted (RISK-1b).** Units are issued or redeemed at one executable valuation per flow (`CashFlow.navBefore`: realized equity plus open positions at their executable value, just before the flow; must be positive or the history is refused), so a flow scales the high-water mark (kept per unit) and the week's base by (navBefore + amount) / navBefore, rounded up. A withdrawal during a drawdown no longer deepens it (E $15 / HWM $20, withdraw $4: the mark becomes $14.67, still 25% down, no kill) and a deposit no longer hides it; a deposit never resets a latched trigger. The weekly limit takes the tighter of the dollar count and the time-weighted count, so no flow loosens it. The daily trigger stays a dollar loss, flow-neutral. At the same instant, trades are applied before flows. The ledger records `navBefore` with each flow (WORKER-1, FUND).
- **2026-10-03 · Withdrawals, first release (RISK-1b).** `evaluateWithdrawal` is the risk check the worker calls before sending SOL to the owner's saved wallet: queued while any position, unresolved entry or reservation is open, or until the wallet and ledger are reconciled; never more than the balance less the live R4 reserve, so the SOL kept for exits, fees, pending transactions and full-loss exposure cannot leave; an unknown or stale balance refuses.
- **2026-10-03 · Day and week boundaries: both figures (RISK-1b).** `dayLoss` / `weekLoss` keep measuring from realized equity at the boundary, so an open loss carried over the boundary is counted again (stricter; kept until the owner changes it, listed with the RISK-1 owner findings). Next to them the snapshot reports `dayChangeMarked` / `weekChangeMarked`: equity now − marked equity recorded at the boundary − net flows since, one valuation at both ends; null when the boundary valuation was not recorded (`AccountHistory.markedAtDayStart` / `markedAtWeekStart`, recorded by the worker at midnight and Monday 00:00 Melbourne time).
- **2026-10-03 · Capital is measured in SOL as well (RISK-1b).** Wallet-marked equity = wallet SOL above the operations floor at the fresh SOL/USD price + open positions at the same marks as ledger equity (the floor is left out so it compares like with like: the bankroll excludes the reserve, R1). Every size and limit that scales with equity uses `capital` = min(ledger equity, wallet-marked equity): R5's risk share (of min(B, capital)), R6(a)'s allowance above the kill line, the weekly room (also never above 20% of current capital, which re-bases it after a withdrawal) and the drawdown step-down. When wallet-marked equity is at or below the kill line, entries stop (`wallet_below_kill_line`, not latched: it lifts with the SOL price). A rise in SOL loosens nothing; a stale or missing price or balance means no entry. `evaluateWithdrawal` is called again right before each send, with a balance read then.
- **2026-10-03 · R10 on economic NAV per unit (RISK-1b, supervisor refinement at 90fac89).** `economicNav` is the one definition, used to price each deposit or withdrawal (`navBefore`) and for the drawdown: wallet SOL above the operations floor at the fresh SOL/USD price plus every open position at its executable mark, gains included. Its high-water mark is the peak of every NAV observed (the worker's `navMarks`, each flow's `navBefore`, and NAV now), scaled by each flow; an owner re-arm restarts it at the first NAV seen at or after the re-arm. The kill switch trips and latches at NAV ≤ 70% of that mark. NAV is taken only when it is consistent: fresh price, balance and marks, no entry in flight, and a balance read at or after the account's latest change; otherwise it is not used, because a close or entry the balance has not seen would read as a loss and latch (entries are refused anyway on stale inputs). The ledger kill line is kept as well, so a rise in SOL never hides a trading loss: either measure trips the switch, R6(a)'s allowance is the smaller room of the two, and a 10% drawdown on either returns sizes to the minimum. Daily and weekly loss stay trading P&L only. Snapshot figures are also reported in SOL (`equitySol`, `capitalSol`, `navSol`, `navHighWaterMarkSol`). Owner finding: with NAV in both directions, a SOL rise followed by a 30% SOL fall latches the kill switch with no trading loss.
- **2026-10-03 · Account costs are not trades (RISK-1b, supervisor).** `AccountHistory.costs` (`{ atMs, amount, kind: 'wallet_setup' }`) holds costs of the account itself, first the wallet's one-time setup rent (WORKER-1 booked it as a fake losing trade, which distorted R8 and trade statistics). A cost lowers realized equity at its time, never raises the high-water mark, and counts in the day's and week's loss (a cost at the day's first instant is that day's); it never enters closed trades, R8, R11, R15 or any trade statistic. A negative amount or a future time is refused (R1), and NAV is taken only from a balance read at or after the latest cost.
- **2026-10-03 · A withdrawal that leaves no positive valuation is refused (RISK-1b review).** A flow with `navBefore + amount ≤ 0` is refused (R1, `bankroll_invalid`): scaling by it would turn the ledger mark, the NAV mark or the week base to zero or below and silence the kill switch.
- **2026-10-03 · Three loss figures per entry (RISK-1b).** Planned R (R5: q × (stop + cost-gate ceiling) + F), stressed executable loss (the emergency rung's min-out below the stop: q × (1 − (1 − s)(1 − e)) + C, $0.80 + C at $2, 20%, 25%), and the reserved loss q + C. Sizing and the reservation stay bounded by the reserved loss, which is always the largest (tested for every stop).

## Rug labels (RUG-1, `packages/core/src/gates/rug-labeller.ts`, `packages/core/src/config/rugs.ts`)

- **2026-10-04 · The rug definition (config `rugs-1`), fixed before the code.** A pump.fun mint is a rug from the first released event, inside its window, at which either rule holds. Its creator is the `creator` of its `CreateEvent`; launch time is that event's chain timestamp. Only events DEC-1 decodes are read: pump `CreateEvent`, `TradeEvent`, `CompletePumpAmmMigrationEvent`, and PumpSwap `BuyEvent`/`SellEvent` on the mint's canonical pool (the pool named by its migration event; other pools are anyone's).
  1. **Creator dump:** the tokens of the mint sold by the creator or by the signer of the create (pump `TradeEvent` with `isBuy` false, PumpSwap `SellEvent` on the canonical pool), summed since launch, reach at least `creatorDump.supplyBps` = **200 bps (2%)** of the create's `tokenTotalSupply`, within `creatorDump.windowMs` = **24 h** of launch. A create without a total supply cannot be judged by this rule; the labeller lists it as unjudged.
  2. **Collapse:** quote liquidity falls to at most `(1 − collapse.dropBps)` of its peak since launch, `collapse.dropBps` = **9,900 bps (−99%)**, within `collapse.windowMs` = **24 h**, with a peak above zero. Liquidity is the curve's real quote reserve after each `TradeEvent` (`realSolReserves`; `realQuoteReserves` for a curve quoted in another mint), then the canonical pool's effective quote reserve (vault + signed virtual reserve, the one that prices trades): pre-trade from every `BuyEvent`/`SellEvent`, and after a sale that less `quoteAmountOut` plus `lpFee` (the LP fee stays in the pool; checked against the CORE-2 golden vectors), so the sale that empties a pool labels at once.

## Fact producers (FACTS-1, `packages/core/src/facts`, `packages/worker/src/facts`)

- **One producer, fed released events only.** `FactProducer.observe` takes every market event in release order and returns the gate facts it changes; `FactFeed` wraps any Feed and releases each fact at its source's moment with id `<source id>~<key>` (sorts right after the source: ids use base58, digits and ASCII punctuation, all below `~`). The engine stores facts like any event, so GATE-1 reads them unchanged and the decision log names each fact's source. Live (WORKER-1) and the backtest (BT-2) wrap their Feed with the same class: same events, same facts (parity test over LiveFeed, `replayRecorded`, `frameEvents` + `createReplay` and DATA-1 raw records).
- **Raw reads are events too.** Live readers never make a fact: they ingest the provider's answer, trimmed, under a raw key (`read:accounts|holders|sim|rugcheck|goplus|jupiter-audit|funder:*`, `sol-usd`, `read:defillama:pump-curve-volume`, `read:exec-health`), and the producer maps it. Recorded answers replay to the same facts.
- **Processed never makes a fact.** Fetched transactions are confirmed; log lines are processed unless the watch was opened at `confirmed` (new FEED-1 `WatchOptions.commitment`, carried in the log frame and its events; its ids and dedup key name the commitment so both copies stay apart). Account reads go through `getMultipleAccounts` at confirmed (TEST-2's reads are processed and stay its own).
- **Nothing proves a negative by absence.** The curve fact exists only from a CompleteEvent (`complete: true`); no trade or silence is read as "not complete". The migration fact needs the CompleteEvent, the migration event and its CreatePoolEvent, all confirmed. A missing vault, a vault that is not the pool's own token account of the right mint, or an undecodable LP mint makes no pool or LP fact.
- **Coverage, not hope, keeps event-built state fresh.** Candles depend on every swap since the pool opened, so their `obs.slot` is the pool's first slot and they carry `stream: trades:<pool>`; `gates/stream:<name>` facts follow FEED-1's `coverage:<name>:start|gap|resume` and `chain:slot` (open gap: flagged `partial`, rejects; bounded gap or a cut/undecodable log on the stream's watch moves `gapFreeSince` past it; resume closes an open gap with no loss). A gap after migration rejects H11 for the rest of the coin's life: strict, and safe.
- **Candle prices** use effective quote (vault + signed virtual) with reserves before the trade from the event and after it by the base amount and the lp-adjusted quote amount (checked on real consecutive swaps: each trade's after equals the next trade's before). Open is the price before a minute's first trade, high the max over before and after prices, close the price after its last trade. A trade whose price cannot be formed, or stamped before the newest candle, flags the candles `partial`. Only the first `chaseCheckAfterMs + 1 min` and the last `candleWindowMs + 1 min` are kept.

## H15 round-trip simulation (SIM-1, `packages/worker/src/sim`)

- **2026-10-04 · One unsigned transaction: the buy at the entry size, then a sell of exactly the quoted tokens,** built with the TX-1 builders for a funded stand-in (TEST-2's approach) after TX-1b's `checkShape`. It is simulated as TEST-2 does: `sigVerify: false`, `replaceRecentBlockhash: true`, `minContextSlot` at the feed head. It is never signed or sent; the no-send import-graph test covers this module too.
  - The sell keeps its token account open. If the buy got more tokens than quoted, the leftover stays and counts as loss; if it got fewer, the sell fails. Both are conservative.
  - The tip transfer is left out of the round trip. It does not touch the venue, so it cannot change the loss. Without it (and its jitodontfront marker), a PumpSwap round trip fits one packet with no lookup table: 1,241 bytes with it, over the 1,232 limit.
- **2026-10-04 · The loss is measured, not modelled.**
  - Loss = −(change in the stand-in's wallet, wSOL and base-account lamports) − the `fee` the node reports − the rent paid into other accounts. The balances are the node's own `preBalances`/`postBalances`, taken inside the simulation.
  - Rent paid is counted narrowly (review of PR #59). A charge must not be booked as rent and drop out of the loss:
    - a new account counts at most the rent-exempt minimum for its size; for wSOL, the rent reserve, capped the same way;
    - the creator vault counts what it gained beyond the creator fees in the stand-in's own TradeEvents (buy and sell), at most its shortfall to rent-exempt;
    - curve growth counts only against a measured length. When the curve is longer after the simulation than at the decision, it is read again (1 credit). A curve that grew in between makes the counted growth smaller, so the loss errs high. The decision-time length is never used to measure.
  - `paid` is the buy leg's own figure: PumpSwap `BuyEvent.userQuoteAmountIn`, or pump `TradeEvent` solAmount + fee + creatorFee. Both are the model's terms, verified by the golden vectors. `proceeds = paid − loss`, so a charge the model misses lowers proceeds and H15 rejects on it.
- **2026-10-04 · Fail-safe outcomes. No fact (H15 rejects the missing evidence):**
  - a payer error (`InsufficientFundsForFee`, `AccountNotFound`, `InsufficientFundsForRent` on account 0). This is the stand-in's funding, not the coin, so it gives "stand-in-unfunded" and drops the funding cache. It never becomes an H15 veto or enters G3's veto-bias count (review of PR #59);
  - unsupported shape;
  - a refused build;
  - an unfunded stand-in;
  - a scheduler refusal ("not evaluated");
  - a timeout, HTTP or RPC error;
  - a malformed answer.
- **2026-10-04 · Fail-safe outcomes. A failed fact (H15 rejects `sim-failed`):**
  - a program error or a stale blockhash;
  - no `fee`;
  - balances missing, or disagreeing with the read-back;
  - no buy event, or a truncated log;
  - paid above the spend;
  - a loss above paid.

  Nothing short of a clean, fully measured simulation passes.
- **2026-10-04 · `checkShape` runs on the buy only.** The sell is built by the same builders for the same market and mint, so it has the same shape (review of PR #59).
- **2026-10-04 · Quota and cost.** Calls run at the shortlisted tier (P2; P0 and P1 are refused before any request).
  - A simulation costs 1 Helius credit. A funding check of the stand-in costs 1 more, at most once per `standInCheckMs`. Both are recorded per simulation, with the latency.
  - FACTS-1's budget counts 1 credit per evaluation for the simulation. The funding check adds 1 per check interval: at 60 s, about one more credit a minute in total, however many candidates there are.
- **2026-10-04 · H15 stays a live-only veto** (the backtest applies the exact local round trip, §16.3). Every simulation returns a `SimRecord` (outcome, reason, credits, latency, measured and modelled loss). WORKER-1 journals it for each shortlisted candidate, next to the H15 reason the gate gives. G3's veto-bias measure (STATS-1b) reads those reasons.

## Backtest review fixes (BT-1c)

- **2026-10-03 · Fill and delay assumptions are harsh and documented (supervisor ruling after external review).** Until measurements justify a change, the fill model keeps harsh values, ordered conservative ≥ base ≥ optimistic (a test enforces the order for every field), with correlated failures and long delays, especially on exits. Reports say "provisional values". The dry run measures feed delay, quote age and simulation behaviour, but it sends nothing, so landing delay and success stay as set through it. They are refined only from observed-chain evidence (landing times of comparable transactions in DATA-1) and later the owner-authorised canary. Each assumption's source and status is in the table at the top of `config/fills.ts`. Promotion reads conservative only.
- **2026-10-03 · Observation delay in three parts.** Event → processed, processed → confirmed (charged because the research config's decision path waits for confirmed), provider → worker. Profiles: `measured` (today's base values, status unmeasured until the worker recorder), `adverse` 2 + 6 slots + 1 s (the conservative scenario), `stress` 4 + 12 slots + 2 s with a 30 s and a 60 s blackout per UTC day. An observation is released at the end of the first block at or after its due slot, at that block's real time; the backlog follows a blackout in chain order; each value carries its chain moment so it never looks fresh; recorded receipt times are never delayed again. Discoveries keep their own drawn lag and go through the same queue, so a blackout holds them too and they arrive in chain order when it ends.
- **2026-10-03 · Exit failures.** Congestion is one network state shared by every open position and every provider: a two-state chain over 150-slot windows. Market activity (all pools' real volume in the previous, complete window, causal) may raise its entry probability up to a cap, but never defines congestion on its own (supervisor correction). With no activity the base state is congested about 9% of windows, not below the earlier 8% burst share. Provider failures per window and deterministic 10/30/60 s bursts drop attempts on top; a dropped attempt is replaced only after its last valid height. Reports give the blocked-exit rate when a whole ladder fell inside congestion (for y_severe) and, with `--burst-sweep`, expectancy and survival against burst frequency.
- **2026-10-03 · Rent follows the account-close outcome.** The final sell of a token account is an atomic sell-and-close. A failed close fails the transaction (sell rolled back, fee charged) and leaves the account sell-only; dust or an unsolicited token (per account) keeps it open. Rent comes back once, only for an entry whose account a landed sell closed. The conservative no-recovery line stays, and economics reports the all-in result with no rent back. `closeSuccessPpm` is an assumption with no refinement source yet (TEST-2's simulations omit the close in some cases and test mechanics, not landing). The close is drawn from a seed per attempt, so scenarios keep common random numbers.
- **2026-10-03 · Drops keep the existing direction.** A dropped attempt costs no fee, so the scenario test keeps conservative ≤ base ≤ optimistic for the drop share (0.2, 0.3, 0.4 of misses). Drops still hurt exits through expiry delay, which the tail, congestion, provider failures and bursts model.
- **2026-10-03 · Liquidity decay on repeated exits is a proceeds haircut.** Each earlier exit attempt on a position takes a share off the next fill's proceeds (counted as slippage) while the pool sees the same sell. A proxy for sellers ahead of us until exit data exist.
- **2026-10-03 · All-in expectancy is per entry decision.** Failed entries cost fees without a trade, so the all-in figure divides by entry decisions (filled or not) as well as by filled trades; the report's mean net stays per filled trade.

Attempt k ≥ 2 is registered only after every earlier holdout is scored or burned; its window starts on the first whole UTC day after registration (a requested start may be later, never earlier), has 28 entry days and the plan's tail length, is written into the attempt at registration, and may not overlap any earlier attempt.
- **2026-10-04 · One holder rebuild (BT-1d, agreed with BT-2).** `dataset/holders.ts` (`HolderBook`) is the only holder rebuild; BT-2 consumes it. It keeps every token account of a mint (owner, amount, delegate, delegated amount, GATE-1e's `HolderAccount` shape), fed in chain order (refused otherwise), and answers as of the last input. A swap credits or debits its `user_token_owner`'s `user_token_account`, never `user` or the signer; a boost buy-and-burn credits nobody; a movement moves between accounts, and a row with an empty owner is left out whole. The mint's holders read `unresolved` (holder facts abstain) from: an empty swap owner, a coverage `unresolved` note once the replay reaches it, activity during the lead-in without movements, a mint searched only in its pump transactions, an owner change or mismatch, or a balance below zero at the end of a transaction. Delegates come from raw account operations through `applyAccountOps`; the handling is stricter than a balance-only rebuild: an account owner change marks the mint unresolved rather than moving the balance silently, and BT-2 keeps holder reads `partial` until DATA-1 keeps every approval-changing transaction. The loader accepts schema 3 only.
- **2026-10-04 · Holder rebuild gives live's account set (BT-1d R1, supervisor ruling).** `holdersAsOf` returns the supply and every account with a balance, the venue accounts included, so the gate sees what live's complete holder read sees. The curve's token account is the curve's real token reserves after each trade plus the tokens it holds back for migration (total supply minus the initial real reserves, from the create), owner the bonding-curve PDA; a migration moves `mint_amount` out of it. A pool's base vault (its associated token account) holds the pool's base reserve after each swap, or its base amount at creation, owner the pool. Supply is the create's total minus burns (boost buy-and-burn included) plus mints. It reads unresolved when no create was seen or the balances do not sum to the supply, mirroring live's `checkHolders`.
- **2026-10-04 · Owner programs from a supplement (BT-1d R2, supervisor ruling).** GATE-1e needs each owner's program to tell a locker from an unknown program. Off-curve owners' programs come from a hash-checked supplement, fetched once with getMultipleAccounts (`scripts/owner-programs.ts`; manifest with its sha256, rows, calls and accounts; the RPC URL is never written). As-of risk: a PDA's owning program is fixed when the account is created and practically never changes, so a later read stands for the decision time. An off-curve owner missing from the supplement leaves its mint unresolved.
- **2026-10-04 · Owner-program supplement workflow (BT-1e).** `.github/workflows/owner-programs.yml`, manual dispatch only, dry run by default. Input: dataset releases `data-FROM-TO`. A `data-day-*` release holds scanner units, not rows, and the assembled release is built only from day releases. Each must be published by github-actions[bot] and match its SHA256SUMS; releases are read one at a time and deleted before the next. The fetch job has contents: read, and HELIUS_API_KEY reaches only its fetch step, as env. The script scrubs the URL, the key and each URL part that can carry a key from every message, and the supplement manifest records the datasets it covers (tag and manifest sha256). The publish job has contents: write, never sees the key, and publishes release `owner-programs-DAY` (fetch day, UTC) under env -i. An existing release is never edited. Builder decision: the input is assembled dataset releases, not raw day releases, because only those hold owner-bearing rows.
- **2026-10-03 · Holdout final form.** The window is [2026-09-22, 2026-10-21) in UTC data days: entries only before the 2026-10-20 cutoff, then a one-day tail (it covers the longest hold any universe may have, plus S0's blocked retries and margin; a test checks it) so every position can finish. `holdout-register` records the window, the cutoff and the tail end before any count is known. One sealed ledger serves every registered universe. Opening (once, after the tail, only with a recorded G1 pass) belongs to the scoring stage; the backtester has no open path.

## Trade-stream gap fills (FILL-2, `packages/worker/src/seed/fill.ts`, `WatchOptions.fill`)

- **2026-10-04 · Why.** H5 reads a pool's trade events since migration, and H11 reads its candles. One gap in `coverage:trades:<pool>` (a restart, a feed drop) rejects that coin for life. Without a fill, every restart drill would permanently reject every pool alive across it, and live would diverge from the backtest, which has every pool row.
- **2026-10-04 · How.** `fillTradeGaps({ rpc, timers, provider, creditCap, gaps, asOf })` takes one `TradeGap` per gap: pool, stream, kind (`position` | `candidate`), `fromSlot`/`fromMs`, `untilSlot` (the live watch's first slot after the gap), the open gap to close (`via`, `fromSlot`), and after a restart `liveStart`. For each gap it calls `getSignaturesForAddress` on the pool over `fromSlot`..`untilSlot − 1` (live covers `untilSlot` onward), then `getTransaction` at `confirmed`. It uses SEED-1's backfill (`backfillAddress`) with the same paging, `minContextSlot`, retry, history-end and as-of rules. Transactions are decoded only through FEED-1's `eventsOfFrame` for a chain-placed `tx` frame, so a filled event has the id, key, position and decoded value the stream gives (tested against the stream path on real transactions). Ranks within a slot follow block order, oldest first.
- **2026-10-04 · After a restart.** The fill returns the events with their chain moments in compareEvents order, to release ahead of the buffered live events. It also returns the close of the saved open gap, dated from the restarted watch's start fact: same slot and position, at `min(fromMs, liveStart.receivedAt − 1)`. That is SEED-1's liveStart rule, tested with the start at `untilSlot`, below it, just after the saved state, and with a clock-skewed receipt time. A complete fill closes with a `resume`, anything less with a bounded lossy gap. The fill reads from the older of `fromSlot` and `close.fromSlot`, so the close never restores a slot it did not read. It is empty and complete (no RPC call) only when that start is after `untilSlot − 1`. `close.at` (when the saved gap was reported) moves the close to just after a saved gap reported at or after the restarted start. Without it, such a gap stays open (fail safe). Both rules are SEED-1's, carried over and tested here. A gap whose `liveStart` or `close.at` is after `asOf` is refused before any call (SEED-1's as-of guard). A close that would still land after `asOf` is not made, so the gap stays open. Events are bounded by `asOf` (slot and receipt time), so nothing after the process start is released. An event held back makes the fill partial. WORKER-1 drops events whose id it already holds from the saved history: ids are deterministic and equal the stream's. Events of other pools in the same transactions come too, as `eventsOfFrame` gives them, without coverage facts of their own. That is harmless under the gap rules: a reader judges a pool only by that pool's own coverage facts, so an extra event can only add to what is known, never close a gap.
- **2026-10-04 · In a run.** FEED-1's socket watch takes an optional `fill` hook. Once a reconnect gap is ready to close (resubscribed, page backfill done, a live slot seen), it asks `fill({ address, fromSlot, toSlot })` for exactly that range and keeps the gap open meanwhile. True closes it with a `resume` (even if the single page alone looked lossy). False or a throw closes it as a bounded lossy gap. A drop while the fill runs discards its answer, and the next connection asks again. `ingestingFill` is the hook body WORKER-1 wires: it fills `fromSlot`..`toSlot` and ingests each transaction into the live feed as a backfilled lookup `tx` frame. The feed places what is already released after it and drops duplicates by signature. An unknown gap start answers false. Without the hook, FEED-1 behaves as before.
- **2026-10-04 · Fail safe and budget.** Until a fill completes, the gap stands and H5/H11 reject. A partial, skipped or failed fill is lossy, never covered. One credit cap per restart (config, passed by WORKER-1) is shared: open positions first at P2, then candidates at P3, oldest gap first. Positions fill at P2, below live open-position monitoring at P1, because exits never wait (supervisor ruling on the #70 review). A test shows a P1 call admitted at once while a long position fill holds its P2 share. Each gap reads at most `maxPagesPerFill` signature pages (default 10, i.e. 10,000 signatures); a busier gap stops as `page-cap`, partial, and stays a gap. A gap reached after the cap is spent is skipped and closed as lossy; a cap that runs out inside a gap stops it there (partial). Each fill reports credits, calls, retries, latency, futures dropped and why it stopped.
- **2026-10-04 · Evidence.** Real PumpSwap transactions from DEC-1's fixtures. Tests: a complete fill makes coverage continuous, a partial fill stays a gap, filled events equal the stream's, nothing is dated after the process start, over budget leaves the gap, the empty fill, and the in-run hook (complete, false, throw, a drop mid-fill, `ingestingFill` into a live feed). 13 of 14 mutations fail the tests. The close's 1 ms margin is tested with the feed's real start id (`coverage:<stream>:start#<seq>`, which sorts before the fill's ids on a tie), here and in SEED-1's tests, which had used an id that hid it (#70 review). The survivor is the hook's "asked once" guard, unreachable today: the socket checks a gap's close once per connection. It is kept as a guard against a double fill.

## Position market from swaps (POS-1, `packages/core/src/facts/producer.ts`, `packages/core/src/fills/pool.ts`)

- **2026-10-04 · Why.** Live, the pool fact came only from account reads, which are made for candidate evidence and the survival mark. Nothing refreshed a held position's pool after entry, so about 2 s later (`maxQuoteAgeMs`) its state was stale: no price stop, trail or take-profit could fire and exits were booked blocked "pool state is stale". Only T_max and the deployer trigger worked. The harness hid it by publishing a pool fact every step.
- **2026-10-04 · One implementation.** The pool after a swap is the backtest's own replay: `replaySwap` (what `ShiftedPool` runs) on the Buy/SellEvent's pre-trade reserves. `realSwap` in core fills is the single mapping from the logged fields to its input; DATA-1's `readAmm` and the live `swapEventState` both use it. `swapEventState` accepts the result only when it reproduces every amount the event logged (base, LP, protocol, creator and buyback fees, quote with LP fee, or quote out); otherwise there is no state. Evidence: on all 353 golden mainnet PumpSwap swaps the event path and the dataset-row path give the same pool and trade to the unit; the state equals the next swap's pre-trade reserves; base and vault match the measured vault balances (`backtest/test/pool-state.test.ts`).
- **2026-10-04 · Sells and the v2 split.** SellEvent does not log its instruction, so a sell replays as v1 (as the dataset rows already do). The price-setting state (base and vault + virtual) is exact either way; after a `sell_v2` the vault is the v1 lower bound (the retained fees sit in the vault and are offset in virtual). The chain check therefore compares base and effective reserve exactly, and the next swap's event reports the real split. A lower vault only makes the sell-exceeds-vault refusal more conservative.
- **2026-10-04 · The chain.** The producer keeps one chain per pool, based on the last coherent account read (its static fields: index, vaults, LP mint, flags, `accountBytes`). Each confirmed swap after the read's slot moves it and releases the pool fact with the swap's provider, slot and receipt time. Swaps at or before the read's slot are already in the read. Processed swaps never move it. A read answered for an older slot than a swap already seen is not released over the newer state.
- **2026-10-04 · Fail closed.** The state goes stale, and the producer releases the last state flagged `partial` with a `stale` reason, when: the pool's `trades:<pool>` stream has not covered every slot since the base (not started by then, an open gap, a bounded gap, or a cut or undecodable log on its watch); a swap's pre-trade base or effective reserve differs from the chain (a swap or a liquidity change not seen: `reserves mismatch`); or a swap does not reproduce its event. Gates reject a flagged fact (`degraded`); the strategy's market refuses it whatever its age ("pool state flagged partial (...)"), so no exit is priced from it; the paper fill and the dry-run build treat it as unknown. Reserves are never guessed. Recovery: after a gap, the first swap the stream carries with no gap from its own slot on re-bases the chain on its pre-trade reserves (the program's own report). After a mismatch only a new coherent read re-bases it (WATCH-1's second-path read is the fallback).
- **2026-10-04 · Pool watches at confirmed.** `PoolWatch` now opens each pool's logs watch at `confirmed` (FACTS-1's `STREAMS.trades` contract; it was processed, which the producer refuses). The deployer-sell trigger dedups by signature, so a processed copy from another watch and the confirmed one count once. Cost: a swap reaches the strategy when its slot is confirmed rather than processed, typically one to a few slots (~0.4–2 s) later; the backtest already charges this processed → confirmed delay on every observation (BT-1c observation delay: adverse 2 + 6 slots + 1 s), so live and backtest decide on the same commitment.
- **2026-10-04 · No gap at entry (review N1).** A pool that becomes held keeps its watch: `PoolWatch` raises it to P1 in place (`RpcStream.setPriority`) instead of re-subscribing, so the trade stream has no gap at entry and the position is priced from the first swap after it. A watch the stream cannot re-prioritise falls back to unwatch and re-watch (a gap, fail closed). Lowered above P1 while the budget halt is on, a watch is dropped as at the halt.
- **2026-10-04 · Base slot and pruning (review N4, N5).** A read's base slot is its pool fact's `obs.slot` (the oldest account it was built from), so a swap after a vault's older read is never taken as already in it. A pool unwatched with reason `not watched` (it left the strategy's list) drops its chain, after releasing its last state flagged. The per-chain dedup set keeps only the newest swap slot's ids; an older swap is refused anyway.
- **2026-10-04 · Known limits.** A quiet pool: the swap-derived fact ages past `maxQuoteAgeMs` like a read, so an exit sent long after the last swap is booked blocked "pool state is stale" until the next swap or WATCH-1's read. Static fields such as `lpSupply` stay those of the last read. Pump curve: TradeEvents already carry post-trade reserves (golden test), and the strategy enters PumpSwap only (curve recorded, not traded), so no curve position needs a market fact; none is built.
- **2026-10-04 · Tests.** `core/test/facts/pool-chain.test.ts` (each swap releases the next state; processed and in-read swaps ignored; gap, hole, late stream, no stream, mismatch, non-reproducing swap; older read), `worker/test/position-market.test.ts` (on the real worker, with the harness no longer publishing pool facts while a position is held, `Market.heldPoolFacts` being the test-only switch for older tests: with no swap the state ages and nothing fires; a 30% sell fires the price stop with no account read after entry; a 15% buy fires take-profit, then a sell fires the trail; a gap makes the state stale and a later sell prices nothing; a flagged fact is never priced from), `worker/test/facts-parity.test.ts` (encoded log lines through FEED-1 and DEC-1: live, the recorded replay and the backtest re-sort give the same pool facts event by event and the same decision log). Each fails on the base. Guard mutations (after review B1, which found three untested guards): the coverage, gap, mismatch (both base-only and effective), mismatch-sticky, older-read, in-read, older-swap re-base (a late swap after a gap), older-swap on a clean chain, dedup, reproduce, prune, strategy flag, paper-market flag, harness switch, in-place priority and halt-on-lower mutations each fail a test (18 of 18); the earlier survivor (a duplicate coverage check on re-base) was removed. Added tests: a late older swap after a bounded gap does not re-base; a delivery twice stays clean, also when the second arrives after newer swaps; a base-only or effective-only mismatch goes stale; a pool leaving the watch list; the base slot from the fact; an exit decided ~2 s after entry is priced and filled, never booked blocked; the watch raised in place keeps coverage through the halt.

## Saved state across restarts (PERSIST-1, `packages/worker/src/persist/state.ts`, `DeployerIndex.snapshot/restore`, `RugLabeller.snapshot/restore`)

- **2026-10-04 · Why.** Without saved state, every boot re-runs the creates backfill. The rehearsal measured 14 boots × a 14-day create backfill ≈ 4,150 credits each, projecting 11.9M Helius credits a month. Saving the deployer index, the labeller's tables and the coverage facts makes the RPC fill run once per host; each later restart tops up only the downtime. The data is public chain data and our own labels only (supervisor approval under the stored-data ruling).
- **2026-10-04 · File.** One JSON file: `{ version, sha256, payload }`. The payload holds the as-of moment, `DeployerIndex.snapshot(asOf, retainFromMs?)`, `RugLabeller.snapshot()` and every released `coverage:*` fact, with bigints written as `{ "$bigint": "…" }`. It is written atomically (a temporary file, fsync, rename), so a crash leaves the old file whole. `saveState` refuses a fact after the moment, or an index taken at another moment.
- **2026-10-04 · As-of honesty.** Nothing in the file may be after its moment: not in event order, and not by receipt time either (a fact with an earlier slot but a later receipt is refused). The index snapshot refuses a moment before its last event. The restore applies the same rule everywhere: the index's first and last moments, its entries and the labeller's launches (`createdAtMs`) may not be after the saved moment, by event order or by receipt time. Pruning entries older than `retainFromMs` moves the index's own start up to that time, so the restored index never claims to have watched what it no longer holds.
- **2026-10-04 · Restore.** `loadState(path, rugConfig, continuing?)` restores the index and the labeller. A labeller saved under another rug config version is refused, because its running sums were judged by other rules. It returns the saved coverage plus, for every started via that was covered at the save, an open `restart` gap at the saved moment. The only exception is the seed's backfill via, which no live stream continues. This holds whether or not the restart continues that watch, so a stream nothing restarts stays not covered (#71 review: excluding it had let it read as covered across the downtime). `continuing(stream, via)` only filters the fill plan. A crash leaves no gap of its own, so without this the downtime would read as covered. It also returns a fill plan: one `RestartFill` per open gap (`stream`, `via`, `fromSlot`, `at`, `synthesized`). A gap already open at the save is planned as it was, not doubled.
- **2026-10-04 · Top-up.** For creates: `buildSeed({ days: [], fill: { fromSlot: asOf.slot, fromMs: asOf.receivedAt, close: { via, fromSlot, at }, liveStart }, … })`, then `index.fill(creates, now)`. For trades: `fillTradeGaps` with the same close. The fill reads only from the saved moment, or an older open gap, to the live start; the test fails if anything before the save is fetched. One fill closes the restart gap, and coverage stays continuous (tested end to end).
- **2026-10-04 · The rug half after a restart.** The `coverage:rugs` restart gap is closed only by something that re-establishes rug coverage over the downtime. The labeller missed the downtime's trades, and no fill reconstructs them for every mint. The named closer is RUG-1c's on-demand per-deployer rug check. Until it exists, the rug half of H14 stays not covered after any restart (fail safe). That makes RUG-1c a dependency of the qualifying dry run with restart drills (supervisor, 2026-10-04).
- **2026-10-04 · Discard.** A missing, corrupt (checksum), truncated, unparsable, version-mismatched or malformed file, or one claiming anything after its moment, is discarded whole. The caller then starts as a fresh process, unseeded and not covered, never "clean".
- **2026-10-04 · Daily budget.** `DailyBudget` keeps the fill's credits per UTC day in its own small file, so it survives a discarded state. It saves on every spend. A missing file is a first boot (the full budget). An unreadable one counts today as spent, because a corrupt budget must not let a reboot loop spend again.
- **2026-10-04 · Evidence.** Tests: a restart restores without re-fetching (index and labeller equal, top-up from the saved moment only, coverage continuous after it), as-of honesty (not covered without the top-up; nothing after the save), an open gap carried as it was, pruning honesty, discards (corrupt, truncated, missing, version, rug config, late fact, late mint, moved moment, bad launch), and the budget. All 21 mutations of the guards fail the tests, the #71 review round included. Since RUG-1c (#74) each label carries its kind (the rule that made it, or null) and each launch its peak venue state. Both are saved and restored exactly, round-trip tested, and a malformed one discards the file. `peakState` must be present. A venue state with a zero peak is refused, because the labeller sets it only when a level beats the peak. A peak with a null state is kept: the labeller produces it for a curve trade without virtual reserves or a pool trade without base reserves (supervisor approval: it adds only fields of public labels the index already holds).
