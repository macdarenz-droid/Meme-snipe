## Order and position lifecycle (CORE-1, `packages/core/src/lifecycle`)

- **2026-10-03 · A failed signature read is terminal only at `finalized`.** A failure read at `processed` or `confirmed` may come from a fork that is later dropped, and the original transaction could still land. Acting on it would allow a replacement, which could mean a second buy or an oversell. Waiting for `finalized` costs about 13 s. A success read counts from `confirmed`: booking a fill early is safe, because the books stay open until every other attempt is dead.
- **2026-10-03 · An attempt is dead only when it has failed at `finalized`, or when the confirmed block height has passed its `lastValidBlockHeight`.** A replacement may be signed, and the books closed, only when every other attempt is dead. Each of these events carries the block height it was read at: `sign_replacement` and `reconcile`.
- **2026-10-03 · Balances are the truth.** A fill found after a failed or expired status is booked with a critical alert. If more than one attempt landed, all are booked (`double_fill` alert). An exit that sold more than its quantity is booked, not refused (`oversold` alert).
- **2026-10-03 · A landing after an intent ended is never refused or left to a person.** This replaces an earlier choice made the same day, which the review rejected. It applies to a landing reported for a cancelled, abandoned or filled intent.
  - The landing raises `unbooked_landing` and a `reconcile_orphan` effect. Every tick repeats the effect until the wallet reconciliation books it with the book's `orphan_fill`.
  - A late buy gets its own open position, so exits can protect it at once. A late sell reduces the position it sold from.
  - New entries stay blocked until it is booked; exits are never blocked.
- **2026-10-03 · A refused reconcile is retried.** While an outcome is known but not reconciled, every tick asks for `reconcile_balances` again, so a reconcile refused because another attempt could still land is retried until it succeeds.
- **2026-10-03 · A late landing reported on a dropped fork is cleared only with finalized proof.** The book's `orphan_cleared` event lifts the entry hold, with an alert, only when all of these hold:
  - balances are unchanged at `finalized`;
  - a `finalized` status read for the signature either failed, or, with a history search, found nothing once the *finalized* block height is past the attempt's last valid height.
  The finalized height matters, because a landing just before expiry is not finalized yet. Without this proof the hold stays, which fails safe. Without the event at all, a fork-dropped report would block entries forever and stop unattended paper runs.
- **2026-10-03 · A restart while `signed` never sends the bytes.** The intent becomes `unknown` and waits for expiry, because we cannot prove whether the bytes left before the restart.

## Engine (ENG-1, `packages/core/src/engine`)

- **2026-10-03 · The engine refuses events outside the total order; a live Feed must release in that order.** Events are ordered by (slot, transaction index, instruction index, receipt time, id). An event that is not after the previous one is logged `out_of_order` and refused, and one dated after now is logged `future_event`. Live, facts can arrive late (a slot-99 transaction after a slot-100 one, or after an off-chain status read in its own slot). So the live Feed holds facts behind a slot horizon and releases them in total order. The parity test replays the *recorded release sequence*, refused events included, rather than re-sorting the raw facts. Re-sorting would deliver facts that live dropped, and the decision logs would differ.
- **2026-10-03 · Repeated reconciles are de-duplicated and rate-limited, and no key waits without bound.** The same `reconcile_balances` or `reconcile_orphan` is sent at most once per `minSlotsBetween`, and at most `maxPerWindow` reconciles go out per `windowSlots`. Under that cap:
  - Orphans (unbooked landings, which block every entry) go before balance reads.
  - Each window reserves max(1, ⌊cap/4⌋) places for waiting balance reads, never more than cap − 1, so exits still reconcile while orphan reads keep failing. With a cap of 1, the two kinds alternate window by window.
  - Within a kind, the key served least recently goes first. A key never served counts as served at its first ask, so a key asked continuously waits at most ⌈K / share⌉ + 1 windows.
  - A waiter that was not asked for in the last slot leaves the line but keeps its turn.

  Earlier versions served first come, first served (which starved orphans), then in rounds by send count (which let returning keys starve a persistent one). This version changes which key is sent compared with those, by design: it is a fairness fix, not a refactor.
- **2026-10-03 · Everything a strategy can reach is frozen.** The book, each decision before it is applied, the effects and the log records are all deep-frozen. A strategy cannot change engine state, or a decision after making it, outside the lifecycle. The log cannot drift from its hash. The replay freezes a copy of its input, never the caller's objects.
- **2026-10-03 · Only the replay driver and its fill model hold a `Replay`.** `momentOf` and `pending` reveal whether future events exist, so neither the engine nor a strategy is given one. The engine gets only the `Clock` and the `Feed`.
- **2026-10-03 · Effect runners are synchronous.** Results come back as feed events. A runner that returns a promise is refused, because results it scheduled after an `await` would arrive after the replay ended and be lost.

## Evidence (`packages/core/src/domain`)

- **2026-10-03 · `checkFreshness` checks age and timestamps only.** It does not reject evidence flagged `fork-suspect`, `provider-degraded`, `partial` or `estimated`, nor evidence read at `processed` commitment. The evidence-gates task must reject these: unknown or degraded evidence is a failure, never a pass. Until that gate exists, `checkFreshness` alone does not prove evidence usable.

## Ledger and storage (LEDGER-1, `packages/core/src/ledger`)

- **2026-10-03 · Outcomes live in a second file.** Labels, the experiment registry and gate results go in a scoring file (`@meme-snipe/core/ledger/scoring`), not in the ledger. The ledger's entry point never imports the scoring module, never attaches a file, keeps its connection out of reach (no property holds it) and refuses to open a scoring file. An import guard test starts from every source file outside `ledger/` and `stats/`, follows static, dynamic, `require`, re-export and package-subpath imports transitively, and fails on any path to the scoring store, a ledger internal or `node:sqlite`. Loads it cannot follow also fail it: a computed `import()` or `require()`, `createRequire`, `process.getBuiltinModule`, native bindings, and any string naming sqlite. Gate results count as outcomes: the worker reads them through the scoring reader, never through the engine.
- **2026-10-03 · Amounts are canonical decimal text.** A u64 token amount can pass SQLite's signed 64-bit integer, so every amount column is `TEXT` with a check (digits only, no leading zero, no sign, no decimal point) and is read back as `bigint`. Label net P&L may be negative (`net_lamports`). Return fractions and probabilities stay `REAL`: they are ratios, not money.
- **2026-10-03 · Append-only by trigger.** Every table refuses `UPDATE` and `DELETE`. Every connection the ledger and scoring code open sets `recursive_triggers = ON`, so `INSERT OR REPLACE` fires the delete guard; upserts fire the update guard. A connection opened outside this code without that pragma could still REPLACE a row: the triggers protect against our own code, not against someone with raw file access. State changes are new rows: intent and position events, a release or keep row per reservation, a done row per outbox item, a result row per operator command.
- **2026-10-03 · One writer by OS lock.** (Replaces a pid lock file, which the review showed two processes could both take.) The writer holds `BEGIN EXCLUSIVE` with `locking_mode = EXCLUSIVE` on a sidecar SQLite file `<file>-writer.lock` for its whole life. The kernel drops the lock when the process dies, so there is no stale-lock handling and no race. A second opener in any process is refused after about 1.5 s of retries. Openers racing for a fresh lock can deadlock inside SQLite: each holds a read lock while waiting for the others to drop theirs, and CI caught a run where all six were refused. So each attempt does not wait. On failure it closes, which drops its read lock, then pauses a random 5–45 ms (`crypto.randomInt`, so racers fall out of step) and retries, up to 60 times. One racer wins. The lock lives in `ledger/adapters/lock.ts`, because pausing the thread and drawing randomness are allowed only in `adapters/` folders. A lock file with foreign content fails closed. Reservation atomicity does not rely on this lock: it runs in `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` and is tested with eight processes on separate connections.
- **2026-10-03 · Times come from the caller.** The ledger never reads the clock, so a backtest on a simulated clock writes the same rows as live. This also keeps it clean under ENG-1's purity guard (PR #10). `schema_migrations` records version, name and checksum, with no applied time.
- **2026-10-03 · A file is stamped with its purpose** (`live`, `paper` or `backtest`) when created, and only opens as that purpose, so a backtest can never write into a live ledger.
- **2026-10-03 · Migrations are forward-only and checked.** Versions run 1..n with no gaps, each in its own transaction, recorded with a SHA-256 of kind, version, name and SQL. A file newer than the code, or one whose applied migration differs from the code's (an edit, or the other kind of file), is refused. Schema 1 holds frozen copies of the domain's venue and status lists; a test fails when the domain lists change, so the change ships as migration 2.
- **2026-10-03 · Telegram may only pause, and issuers are roles.** The schema refuses any other command with auth level `telegram` (docs/ARCHITECTURE.md §12), and `issued_by` must be `owner` or `system`, so no Telegram id or email can be stored.
- **2026-10-03 · Node version.** `node:sqlite` is unflagged from 22.13, and the crash tests run child processes with type stripping (unflagged from 22.18). The root `engines` field is `>=22.18`; CI's `node-version: 22` resolves to 22.22.
- **2026-10-03 · Reservation limits fail closed.** A missing or malformed limit (`maxHeld` not a bigint ≥ 0, `maxCount` not a safe integer ≥ 1) throws and writes nothing, never lets a reservation through.
- **2026-10-03 · The ledger stores intent statuses; it does not judge them.** The CORE-1 reducer is the authority on legal sequences. A status-only check in the ledger would refuse legal ones, such as a late landing after `reconciled`. Accepted by review on condition that the replay check is tracked by name: **LEDGER-REPLAY**, under pre-funding gate item 2. LEDGER-REPLAY replays every stored intent and position event through the CORE-1 reducer and fails on any illegal sequence. To be built with BT-1 (it needs the stored events with their full payloads) and added to the task board by the supervisor.
- **2026-10-03 · Guard rules match ENG-1's.** ENG-1's purity guard (PR #10) forbids I/O, clocks, randomness, dynamic `import`/`require` and global escape hatches in core outside `adapters/` folders, which it skips entirely. It has a named `ledger/` exemption that allows `node:sqlite`, and it checks every string in `ledger/` for SQL that reads the clock or randomness, or that attaches or detaches a file. So the ledger's SQL stays in `ledger/`, where that check runs (an earlier move of it into `ledger/adapters/` was reverted because it took the SQL out of the check). Only the writer lock, which needs `Atomics` and `randomInt`, is in `ledger/adapters/lock.ts`, and it holds no ledger SQL. Since ENG-1 merged, its guard applies that SQL check to every string in `ledger/**`, `adapters/` included, so the ledger keeps no separate SQL test (one was used until then and removed as a duplicate). The ledger's import guard adds the outcome-store rules: no `node:sqlite`, `getBuiltinModule`, `createRequire`, computed import or require, native bindings or sqlite-naming string outside `ledger/` and `stats/`. Checked against the merged guard (base 3f080cf). Limit: no static scan stops code written on purpose to get around it (for example a module name built at run time behind `globalThis[...]`; ENG-1's global-escape-hatch rule catches that particular form). The guards cover every ordinary way to load a module; deliberate evasion is for code review to catch.

### Ledger replay check (LEDGER-REPLAY, `packages/core/src/ledger/replay`, `pnpm ledger:replay`)

- **2026-10-03 · Book events are stored in the existing `detail` column, with no new table (approved by the supervisor, same day).** The ledger had no book-event log, and `intent_event.seq` and `position_event.seq` are separate sequences. `intent_event.seq` is the global order. Each stored book event writes one intent row per intent the reducer persists, in effect order. The first row carries `detail = {"v":1,"book":<BookEvent>}`, and later rows of the same event carry `null`. A `created` row stands for `propose_entry` (entry) or `trigger_exit` (exit), whose reasons are kept in that step's `position_event.detail` in the same versioned form. An event that persists no intent but changes the book or a position writes one row, with the status unchanged, for the intent it concerns: a landing reported for an ended intent, `orphan_cleared`, or a trigger merged into a running exit. `stepRows` decides the rows, so writers and the replay share one rule. BT-1 and the worker must write this way; `test/ledger/recorder.ts` shows how.
- **2026-10-03 · The stored event is versioned and checked strictly (supervisor's condition).** `encodeBookDetail` (on write) and `decodeBookDetail` (on read), in `ledger/replay/schema.ts`, refuse an unknown or missing version, a missing or extra field at any depth, a wrong type, a non-canonical id or signature, and a negative or non-bigint amount. Events the replay rebuilds from the intent and position tables pass the same check. A new field or event type needs version 2.
- **2026-10-03 · Not stored, not checked:** pause and resume, a restart that changes no intent, and ticks. They only refuse entries, so leaving them out can make the replay more permissive but never changes a replayed state. The default open-position limit is "none" for the same reason. `--max-open-positions` checks a session's limit.
- **2026-10-03 · Read-only means the ledger bytes never change.** The replay opens the file with `openLedgerReader` (SQLite read-only). On a WAL file, SQLite still creates its empty `-wal` and its `-shm` lock index beside the file. These hold no ledger data, and the tests check that the `-wal` stays empty and the file is unchanged byte for byte.
- **2026-10-03 · Stamps.** The file's purpose stamp decides which decision modes it may hold: `backtest` holds only `replay`, `paper` holds `paper` and `shadow`, and `live` holds `live` and `shadow` (shadow runs beside paper and live, quant.md §8). The command refuses files with different stamps in one run.
- **2026-10-03 · Compaction.** The CORE-1 book copies its maps on every event, so a replay of the whole book slowed with the square of the ledger's size (1.9k events a second at 2.5k intents). The replay moves ended intents and closed positions out of the book every 64 events. It brings one back before any event that names it (by intent id, idempotency key, position id or orphan signature). The reducer reads the others only to skip them, so states are unchanged. A test compares the compacted and uncompacted reports on honest and tampered ledgers. The engine pays the same quadratic cost during a backtest, which is a risk for BT-1's throughput target. The replay also reads `fill` and `attempt` once each, grouped by intent, because `fill` has no index on `intent_id` and a read per intent scanned the whole table. Measured on engine-generated backtest ledgers: 22.2k events a second at both 21k and 63k events (7.4k intents), so the cost now grows linearly.
- **2026-10-03 · Whole tables, not only known intents (review of 611a4bb).** A fill, attempt, reservation or reservation event for an intent that does not exist would pass a check that reads rows only by known intent ids. The replay therefore also runs `PRAGMA foreign_key_check` (read-only) and requires the `fill`, `attempt`, `reservation` and `reservation_event` totals to equal the replayed totals. Each table has a test that fails on 611a4bb.
- **2026-10-03 · Named allowance: "held while the outbox row is pending".** When the reducer has released or kept a reservation, the stored reservation may still read held while the `release_reservation` or `keep_reservation` outbox row for that intent is not yet done. The runner ends the reservation after the state change commits. Once that outbox row is done, a reservation still held fails the check. Tests cover both cases.
- **2026-10-03 · Not compared by the replay:** the contents of `fee`, `outbox` (beyond the allowance above), `outbox_done`, `decision`, `feature_snapshot`, `observation` and `operator_command`, and every event and row timestamp (`ts`, `created_ts`). The reducer produces none of these, so there is nothing to replay them against. Their stamps and foreign keys are still checked.
- **2026-10-03 · LEDGER-1b: many positions per entry intent (migration 2; supervisor approved the data shape).** A buy that lands after its entry ended gets its own position (`<position>.o<n>`) with the same entry intent, so `position.entry_intent_id` is no longer `UNIQUE`. The position table is rebuilt with the same columns and checks, a plain index on `entry_intent_id`, and its append-only triggers restored. Rows are copied out and back, not renamed, because `ALTER TABLE RENAME` opens SQLite's temp schema on the writer's connection, and the label guard requires that connection to hold only `main`. A migration that rebuilds tables sets `rebuildsTables`. The runner then switches foreign keys off before `BEGIN` (SQLite's 12-step ALTER) and runs `foreign_key_check` before commit, rolling back on any orphan. It switches foreign keys back on whatever happens. The flag is not part of the checksum, so migration 1's checksum is unchanged. Before this fix, a late buy could not be stored and the replay failed it with table `position`. It is now stored and replays.

## Configuration and policy (CFG-1, `packages/core/src/config`)

- **2026-10-03 · Code loads limits and tightens them; it never raises them.** A session starts only from a policy that is tighter than or equal to an approved baseline on every field. Baselines are a frozen list in `baselines.ts`, starting with the trial preset. Raising a limit means adding a baseline in a reviewed pull request (the owner's step); nothing at runtime can add one.
- **2026-10-03 · Every field has a direction.** `max` (a cap or trigger: may fall only), `min` (a floor: may rise only), `locked` (any change needs a new version), `free` (the label). A new field without a direction does not compile.
- **2026-10-03 · The exit path is locked.** Exit fees, the fee ceiling, min-out and the emergency rung cannot be changed by override, and validation refuses a zero fee or a zero slippage allowance on any rung. A lower number there can stop an exit landing, so it is never "tighter".
- **2026-10-03 · A saved policy is read by a strict reader.** Duplicate keys and `__proto__`, `constructor` and `prototype` keys are refused; every field is checked and covered by the hash; a field supplied through a prototype is not a field. The session copies, then validates, then locks the copy.
- **2026-10-03 · Values deferred to the cards that own them** (new policy schema version when they land): the H15 round-trip tolerance, the regime's execution-health thresholds and entry slippage (GATE-1 and RISK-1). The H13 dev-cluster cap (5%) and the H14 14-day rug look-back are in the policy now.
- **2026-10-03 · The money-literal scan reads tokens.** It knows regex literals and decodes string escapes. It flags any number of 1,000 or more in any notation, any nonzero literal in a statement with `MICRO_PER_USD` or `LAMPORTS_PER_SOL` or inside a money constructor call, numeric strings given to `BigInt`, `Number`, `parseInt` and `parseFloat`, and money constructors built from a literal, anywhere in `packages/core/src` outside `config/`. The allow-list is by exact file, declared name and value, and a test fails if an entry goes stale. Limit: an amount assembled from small numbers is not seen.

## Evidence gates (GATE-1, `packages/core/src/gates`)

- **2026-10-03 · The gates read facts only through the as-of store and judge each one before use.** Facts have fixed keys and shapes (`gates/facts.ts`). A fact is unusable, and the gate that needed it rejects under H16 with a typed reason, when it is missing, malformed, stamped after now, flagged anything other than `backfilled` or `deduplicated` (so `fork-suspect`, `provider-degraded`, `partial`, `estimated` and `rate-limited` all reject), or, for chain facts, read at `processed` commitment or without a commitment. This closes the gap noted under Evidence above.
- **2026-10-03 · Freshness is "2 slots or 2 s", from the policy (`maxStateSlotLag`, `maxQuoteAgeMs`).** Chain state read once (live `getTokenLargestAccounts`) must be within 2 slots of now. State kept current by a subscription (or the backtest dataset) names its stream: it stays fresh while that stream's head is within 2 slots of now and the stream has had no gap since the value was observed. A dataset gap therefore rejects a candidate, never fills it. Off-chain reads (simulation, third-party checks, execution health) must be at most 2 s old. Events that never change (create, migration) need only be present.
- **2026-10-03 · Live-only vetoes are absent in the backtest, as §16.3 says, not "unknown".** The GATE-1 task card said a live-only input rejects in the backtest unless §16.3 gives a proxy. §16.3 gives one for H15 (the exact local round trip) and says the H16 cross-checks and execution health are absent, with the bias written into every report, because requiring them would reject every historical candidate. The gates follow §16.3: in the backtest they are skipped and recorded as `live-only-not-applied` notes, so BT-1 can count them for the bias note. Every other input is required in both modes.
- **2026-10-03 · H6 rejects any outstanding LP token.** Migration LP is burned, so a canonical pool has no LP outstanding: the LP mint supply was 0 on all four canonical pools in DEC-1's mainnet fixtures (read 2026-10-03). Any LP outstanding is liquidity someone can withdraw. No threshold, so no new policy field.
- **2026-10-03 · H8 compares the quote side alone with the R12 floor.** Effective quote reserve (vault + signed virtual reserve) valued at the latest hourly SOL/USD point, rounded down, against max(floor, 1,000 × size), and the U1 floor for U1. This is the literal reading of H8 and half of the two-sided "liquidity" figure, so it is the stricter one. A SOL/USD point older than 2 h (two steps of an hourly series) is stale.
- **2026-10-03 · H11 needs 1-minute candles.** Any other interval is malformed: shorter candles would split a spike into small steps.
- **2026-10-03 · H11 uses the candle high.** A 1-minute candle in the last 3 minutes whose high is more than 25% above its open rejects (stricter than the close). The +5 min price (U2 only) is the close of the last candle that ended by migration + 5 min; none means unknown.
- **2026-10-03 · H12 and H13 measure shares of circulating supply, rounded up.** Circulating = supply − excluded balances. Excluded, by name only: this mint's curve accounts, the canonical pool's stored base vault (by address, not every account the pool owns), the mayhem vault (owner PDA ["sol-vault"] of `MAYHEM…`, checked equal to `BwWK17cb…`) and the incinerator. Locker accounts stay holders and are noted (review round 2): the Raydium locker escrows LP tokens and position NFTs, never a coin's own tokens, and its immutability is unproven. A locker is excluded only under a later decision with evidence: an immutable or pinned program, holding this mint's tokens, locked past our holding time. Any other PDA owner stays in the holder set and is noted, even one of pump's or PumpSwap's own programs: a non-canonical PumpSwap pool is a PumpSwap PDA whose LP owner can withdraw its tokens (review round 1; an earlier blanket "program account" exclusion let a whale hide there). The holder read's supply must equal the mint's, or H12 and H13 reject as inconsistent. Holders are grouped by owner. On a largest-accounts list, an insider who is not listed counts at the smallest listed balance (an upper bound). The dev counts in both the insider total and the dev cluster.
- **2026-10-03 · H14 windows.** Serial: distinct mints by the creator with creation time in the last 24 h, this mint included, more than `serialMaxMints24h`. Rug: a prior rug the index learned of within the last `deployerRugLookbackDays` (inclusive). The index must be gap-free since at least that long ago, otherwise H14's input is not covered and rejects.
- **2026-10-03 · H15's tolerance is the integer rounding bound, so no policy field is needed now.** Live, the simulated round trip may lose at most the local model's loss plus `ROUND_TRIP_ROUNDING_LAMPORTS` (16 lamports). This is the strictest tolerance that exact math allows. A looser one would be a new policy field (CFG-1 deferred it to this card); none is added.
- **2026-10-03 · The regime gate checks on the hour, with the policy's hysteresis.** The current check is the latest hour boundary that the SOL/USD series and the graduate snapshot have both reached, and it must be within 2 h of now. Each check evaluates graduate survival (share of graduates whose +30 min mark fell in the last 24 h with effective reserves above 30 SOL, against the exact median of the 14 daily shares before), curve volume (last full UTC day against the nearest-rank 25th percentile of the 365 days ending with it) and SOL's 24 h change in basis points (above −800). Inside a snapshot, points dated after the check are ignored. The gate is off at once when the current check cannot be computed. Otherwise it is off when the last `failedChecksToDisable` checks all failed (an unknown earlier check counts as failed). Live, a missing, stale or red execution-health fact turns it off; in the backtest that input is absent.
- **2026-10-03 · Soft features are logged, never scored yet.** Values come from the feed's precomputed soft fact, the holder exclusions and our deployer index. Unknown values stay unknown with the reason. RugCheck's single-holder flag is logged as false when the largest account is a known vault. Third-party fields (RugCheck score and flag) are logged as live only in the backtest, whatever the feed supplies. A rule score needs thresholds that only calibration (§13) can give.
- **2026-10-03 · Evaluation order is fixed and cheapest first.** Field checks first (H1–H4, H6–H11, H14, H16), then address derivation and holder classification (H5, H12, H13), then the quote and simulation (H15). By default evaluation stops at the first failing gate; calibration runs can evaluate all. Each fact is looked up once per evaluation. Holder order in the input does not change the result.
- **2026-10-03 · Time units live in `config/time.ts`.** The money-literal guard bans numbers of 1,000 or more outside `config/`, so the gates import minute, hour and day lengths from there.
- **2026-10-03 · GATE-1b: our own deployer index, fed only with released events.** `DeployerIndex` is held by the strategy and observes every market event the engine releases, so it is as of now by construction; each answer is also filtered to now. It counts each creator's mints from FEED-1's create events (`logs:pump:CreateEvent:<mint>` from the logs stream and `pump:CreateEvent:<mint>` from fetched transactions, one count per mint at its earliest block time) and rug labels from `rug:<mint>` facts `{ mint, creator }`, each dated when it was recorded. Processed-commitment creates are counted: a create that is later rolled back can only raise the count, never lower it. No rug labeller exists yet, and §7 gives no rug rule, so none is invented here. Rug labels count only once a reviewed labeller is wired by an explicit flag (`GateDeps.rugLabeller: 'RUG-1'`), never because some `rug:` fact exists, and only while its own `coverage:rugs:*` facts show it covered the whole look-back (same rules as creates). Until then every evaluation that reaches H14, live and backtest, carries the note `rug-labels-unavailable` ("rug labels unavailable (no reviewed labeller; RUG-1)") for BT-2 to count, the prior-rug half is not applied, and the soft feature `indexRugs` is unknown: no labels is never read as zero rugs. The deployer-count half stays enforced (supervisor, 2026-10-03).
- **2026-10-03 · H14 checks creates coverage itself, whatever built the index.** It reads the as-of history of FEED-1's `coverage:creates:start|gap|resume` facts (several watches share each key). Coverage begins at a start, which must be reported by the look-back start. An open gap (`toSlot` null) stays uncovered until a bounded gap or a resume with the same `via` and `fromSlot`, or a new start on the same `via`. A bounded (lossy) gap reported at or after the look-back start is uncovered. A resume restores the range. Unreadable coverage facts count as open gaps. Gaps from every watch count, even if another watch was up (the safe side). Any of these rejects as H16 `not-covered` (input `coverage`), so a gap can never make H14 count fewer mints. Ranges are judged by when they were reported, because the gate has no slot-to-time map: a gap cannot end after its report. The backtest feed must emit the same coverage facts (dataset gaps as bounded gaps).
- **2026-10-03 · The index must have watched the whole look-back itself.** `DeployerIndex` records its first observed event; its `coverageFromMs` is the later of that and the stream's coverage. An index started mid-run (a restart, or one built late) has not seen the creates before it, so H14 rejects as not covered even when the stream's coverage facts say covered (review of PR #29).
- **2026-10-03 · A cut or undecodable creates log is a hole until its transaction is fetched.** FEED-1 marks a cut log (`truncated: true` on its events) and emits `logs:truncated:<via>` or `logs:undecodable:<via>` when no event could be read. On a creates watch (a `via` named by a `coverage:creates:start`), any of these inside the look-back makes H14 reject as H16 `not-covered` (input `coverage`) until a fetched-transaction event for the same signature (`ev:<signature>:…`) is released.
- **2026-10-03 · An unreadable coverage fact blocks H14 until an operator acts.** It counts as an open gap, so H14 rejects every deployer (fail safe). The operator path: fix the producer, then restart the creates watch so FEED-1 reports a new `coverage:creates:start` on that `via`; that settles the gap as lossy, and H14 passes again once the look-back (14 days) has moved past it, or at once after a SEED-1 backfill covers the range. Nothing in code clears it.
- **2026-10-03 · SEED-1 (design only, a card for WORKER-1's wave): seeding the deployer index at start-up.** With the index's own start checked, a fresh or restarted worker would reject H14 for 14 days, including the 48 h qualifying dry run. So at start-up, before the live streams are trusted, the index is seeded through `DeployerIndex.seed(events, coverage)`. `events` are create events in the same shape FEED-1 emits; `coverage` are `coverage:creates:*` facts for the seeded range, read with the same rules. Sources, oldest first: (1) DATA-1's published days of create transactions (no RPC cost), then (2) an RPC backfill from the dataset's last slot to the process start, using `getSignaturesForAddress` on the pump program's create authority and `getTransaction` at `confirmed` for each signature (about 50k creates a day, so roughly 2 calls per create: about 100k calls for a one-day catch-up, far more for longer; the scheduler runs it at P3 within the 70% credit rule and stops rather than exceed it). As-of rules: seeding happens before the engine's first decision, and every seeded event and coverage fact is dated at or before the process start, so nothing seeded is newer than the first decision moment. Seeded events count from their block time, and the index's own start becomes the seeded range's start. Backfill gaps (a page limit hit, a failed call, a slot range with no answer) are reported as bounded lossy `coverage:creates:gap` facts for that range, so a partial seed stays uncovered there and is never read as complete. The live stream's own `coverage:creates:start` then continues coverage from the process start.
- **2026-10-03 · The create fact can come from FEED-1.** When no `gates/create` fact exists, H9, H12, H13 and H14 read FEED-1's create from a fetched transaction (confirmed) or, failing that, from the logs stream. The logs stream is subscribed at `processed`, so a create known only from logs is refused (H16 degraded) until the transaction is fetched at `confirmed`.

## Funding screens (FUND-1, `apps/web/src/funding`)

- **2026-10-03 · What can be withdrawn.** Balance minus the protected SOL reserve (§8 R4, from the worker) minus one transfer fee (5,000 lamports), never below zero. The fee comes off the amount that can be sent so a full send never dips below the reserve. SOL amounts are decimal strings with up to 9 places and all arithmetic is bigint lamports; AUD values use the SOL/AUD price string and round to cents.
- **2026-10-03 · One destination.** The Withdraw form refuses any address that is not exactly the saved wallet, and a malformed one is reported as malformed. The code is drawn black on white in both themes because scanners read dark on light.
- **2026-10-03 · Requirements for SIGN-1.** (a) After the passkey step-up, the signer path re-checks the destination against the latest saved wallet and the amount against the latest balance and reserve, and the signer re-checks both again; the screen's check is a convenience, not the control. Persisting it is a new saved-data shape, so it needs the owner's approval together with SIGN-1.
- **2026-10-03 · A throwing step-up counts as a refusal.** The step-up runs inside a promise chain, so a synchronous throw, a rejection or any answer other than `true` all mean "not approved", and the busy flag is cleared in a `finally`.
- **2026-10-03 · Deposit shows no address before the gate passes.** Kept from WEB-1 (CLAUDE.md, "No deposit before proof"): until the pre-funding gate passes, Deposit shows "Not passed" and no address or QR. The sample-data page passes the gate.

## Transactions and landing (TX-1, `packages/core/src/tx`)

- **2026-10-03 · Wallet-owned accounts and transfer destinations are always static keys.** Venue and infrastructure accounts may load from a lookup table; the wallet, its token accounts, its volume accumulators and the tip account never do. The signer cannot check what a table resolves to, so the policy refuses any of those loaded from one.
- **2026-10-03 · The signer policy recomputes the worst-case SOL out from the decoded bytes.** Base fee, priority fee (from the compute-budget instructions), every transfer out, the curve buy's spend, the larger of the wrap transfers and the PumpSwap buy's `spendable_quote_in` (wrapped SOL already in the wallet's ATA would be spent too; anyone can send some), and each account the swap may create or top up, charged at the rent of the largest such account (170 bytes) at the live rate, never a caller's figure. An account created and closed in the same transaction is not charged. The builder's own itemised figure must never exceed it (review of PR #20).
- **2026-10-03 · A transaction that names any account twice is refused,** by the policy and by DEC-1's `accountKeys`, as the runtime refuses it (AccountLoadedTwice) (review of PR #20).
- **2026-10-03 · A buy quote carries the spend it was computed for; it must equal the request's spend, with at most 3 lamports left unspent by fee rounding** (measured 0 on 40,000 random quotes). A quote made for a smaller spend would size min-out for the wrong trade (review of PR #20).
- **2026-10-03 · The PumpSwap buyback recipient's quote ATA is charged when not read as existing.** All eight exist on chain (slot 452960110) and the IDL does not list them as created, but the program is not open source.
- **2026-10-03 · Rent is charged at SIMD-0437 rates read live.** Token-2022 pump ATAs are sized 170 bytes, SPL accounts 165, volume accumulators 137; an old bonding curve is charged for growth to 151 bytes (pump-sdk 2.0.0; the pinned BUY.md says 115, the larger is used).
- **2026-10-03 · The jitodontfront marker rides on the tip transfer, read-only.** The System program ignores extra accounts; venue programs parse their remaining accounts by position, so the marker is never added to a swap.
- **2026-10-03 · Compute limits come from a calibration table (p99 × 1.1, at least 20 samples).** The provisional table is built from 60 real mainnet swaps per kind with only our instruction set; TEST-2 replaces it with our own simulations. A shape missing from the table refuses the build. A priority fee that rounds to a zero unit price is refused, since Sender requires a price instruction.
- **2026-10-03 · Fee recipients come from the chain-read Global and GlobalConfig, picked by a seeded index.** The same inputs always build the same bytes.
- **2026-10-03 · A broadcast sends only the stored bytes, after checking they carry the attempt's signature and blockhash and are fully signed.** Sender (SWQoS-only, mev-protect) and our RPC get the same bytes with `skipPreflight` and `maxRetries: 0`. A path that answers with another signature is an error. Replacement waits for CORE-1's expiry, reconciliation and dead-attempt proof, checked again before anything is built.
- **2026-10-03 · A status check reads the confirmed slot and block height first, in one `getEpochInfo` answer, and requests the statuses only after it answers; the events carry that height, and a status answer counts only if its `context.slot` is at least that slot** (one RPC URL can be served by several nodes; a lagging node's not-found proves nothing). CORE-1 expires an attempt on "not found after a history search" past lastValidBlockHeight. A height read after (or alongside) the statuses can be newer than the status snapshot, so a transaction landing in between would be expired and replaced: a double trade (review of PR #20).
- **2026-10-03 · The policy charges a created Token-2022 ATA at 170 bytes; it cannot see the mint's extensions.** A mint with size-changing account extensions (transfer fee and the like) makes a larger ATA, so a transaction built outside the builder could be undercounted by about 60k lamports. Only the builder refuses those mints. SIGN-1 may tighten this by taking the mint account (review of PR #20).
- **2026-10-03 · The policy's worst case for a PumpSwap trade includes the buyback recipient's quote ATA as a flat charge,** about 1.5M lamports (one 170-byte account at 5,080 lamports per byte) above the builder's figure when that account exists. SIGN-1's `maxSolOut` needs that headroom (review of PR #20).

## Live feed and quota scheduler (FEED-1, `packages/worker/src/providers`, `packages/worker/src/scheduler`)

- **2026-10-03 · A live transaction's position in its slot is its rank by first arrival.** No live source gives a transaction's index in its block, so the live Feed sets `txIndex = 2^32 + rank`, ranking each signature in its slot by the arrival order (`seq`) of its first frame. A later arrival never changes an earlier rank, so a late frame cannot reorder what was released. Inside a transaction, `ixIndex` is 0 for the sighting and `1 + outerIx × 2^16 + innerIx` for each decoded event, so events keep execution order and a transaction stays contiguous. Account states sort after every transaction of their slot, then the slot notice, then off-chain facts. The same mapping (`frameEvents`) serves the backtest Feed over recorded frames.
- **2026-10-03 · The live Feed fixes each frame's place at receipt, and the recorder keeps it.** A stream fact sits at its own slot. A fact with no slot (PumpPortal), a Parsed Streams report (delivered at `confirmed`, usually after its slot is released), and an answer to our own lookup that comes after its slot was released are placed off-chain in the open slot, the first one not yet released. Without this, every on-demand decode would arrive late and be refused. Frames record their duplicate flag too, so the backtest Feed reproduces live dedup exactly, even after dedup memory is pruned. Off-chain event ids carry the frame's `seq`, so a copy that returns after its dedup key was forgotten still gets a unique id.
- **2026-10-03 · Late stream facts follow the ENG-1 rule, and the release sequence is recorded.** A stream fact for a slot already released is released at once, marked late, and the engine refuses it as `out_of_order`. Every event handed to the engine produces a `Release` record {index, frameSeq, eventId, late} that the recorder keeps with the frames. The parity replay of recorded live data (`replayRecorded`) feeds exactly that sequence, on a clock that moves as the live one did. Re-sorting the frames (`frameEvents` with `createReplay`) would accept what live refused, so it is kept only for data with no release record (review of PR #21, supervisor ruling). Chain facts older than `keepSlots` behind the release point enter off-chain instead, so ranks and dedup keys can be forgotten without changing any later release.
- **2026-10-03 · Transactions are decoded only by DEC-1, and creates are read from their log lines.** A `logsSubscribe` notification is a sighting of its signature. A fetched transaction is decoded only by DEC-1's `transactionEvents`. The creates stream (`decodeLogs`) also keeps its log lines as their own frame, read by DEC-1's `logEvents`. So every create's creator, mint and slot reach the H14 deployer index at no RPC cost, even when PumpPortal's copy won dedup. Log events sort after a transaction's instruction events, carry a `truncated` flag, and a cut or malformed log becomes a `logs:truncated` or `logs:undecodable` fact, never a guess. Sightings filled by backfill carry no logs, so a watch named for coverage (`coverage: 'creates'`) reports `coverage:creates:start` {fromSlot} when first subscribed and `coverage:creates:gap` {fromSlot, toSlot, reason} for every range it may have missed: each reconnect when it reads logs, otherwise a backfill that failed or filled a whole page; `toSlot` is null when the watch is unwatched, halted or refused. A gap starts at the watch's own last log-notification slot, inclusive (slot notices run ahead of logs, so creates of that slot may still have been in flight). The gap is reported open (`toSlot` null) at the moment of the drop, so decisions made during an outage, or a stream that never recovers, see the range as uncovered. When it closes it is settled by a bounded gap with the same `fromSlot`, or by `coverage:creates:resume` when backfill restored it in full. It ends at the first slot this stream sees live after the resubscribe, never at another provider's tip, and only once that connection's backfill has finished. Every drop and open moves a connection epoch, so a backfill from a lost connection can never close a newer gap. After `resetBudget` the stream accepts watches again, and a new start fact marks where coverage resumes (review of PR #21). GATE-1's deployer index marks those ranges uncovered and H14/H16 reject deployers whose history touches them, never counting fewer mints (supervisor, 2026-10-03). Decoding every create is not affordable: about 50k creates a day is about 1.5M `getTransaction` calls a month, 150% of Helius Free's credits or twice Alchemy Free's 30M CU. So the worker fetches only where it decides to: migrations (needed for `CompletePumpAmmMigrationEvent`), shortlisted tokens and the open position. Every other create stays a sighting.
- **2026-10-03 · Rate buckets are sliding windows.** Provider limits are windows: Jupiter documents a 60 s sliding window, and Helius counts requests per second. A refill-rate token bucket either bursts past such a window or wastes part of it. Each provider bucket grants at most `limit` per window, keeps `floors[c]` grants free for higher classes, and has optional caps by class or lane. RugCheck runs at one request per 4.5 s, the only rate verified never to hit a 429.
- **2026-10-03 · At 70% of the monthly credits only P0 requests run, and the open position stays watched.** New requests of P1–P3 are refused, P2–P3 stream watches are dropped, and new P1–P3 watches are refused. Existing P0–P1 watches stay open. The card says all non-exit traffic halts, but the open position's stream is what triggers its exit. Closing it would leave an exit with no fresh state, so keeping it is the safe reading of "exits are never starved". Stream bytes are still metered.
- **2026-10-03 · The month's credit use is loaded at start and reported on every spend.** A restart must never reset the 70% halt. The scheduler takes `creditsUsed` and calls `onSpend` with each new total. Persisting it is WORKER-1's job.
- **2026-10-03 · A reconnect holds the release point below the missed slots for up to 3 s.** While a stream's backfill runs (`getSignaturesForAddress` since the last seen signature for each log watch, `getAccountInfo` for each account watch; always at P0 for P0–P1 watches, because they serve the open position and the 70% halt must not starve them), the release point stays below the first slot it may have missed. Backfilled facts therefore arrive on time and are marked `backfilled`. After 3 s the hold ends, and later backfill answers follow the late rule. With no tip progress for 2 s, everything held is released (a stale feed never blocks the engine), and a `feed:status:<provider>` fact reports each drop, refusal and recovery.
- **2026-10-03 · The worker imports core by relative path.** That adds no dependency and no lockfile change. Switching to a `workspace:*` link is the supervisor's call.

## Backtest and paper fills (BT-1, `packages/backtest`, `packages/core/src/fills`)

- **2026-10-03 · Our trades change the pool every later trader sees.** The dataset gives each real PumpSwap swap's pre-trade reserves. Without our trades, the pool after a swap is that swap replayed on its own pre-trade state with the exact CORE-2 math. Once our trade lands, the pool carries a delta; each later real swap is replayed on (pre-trade + delta) with its own input (exact base, exact quote in, or base sold), and the delta becomes what remains after it. Pre-trade reserves in each event resync the real state, so liquidity and boost changes between swaps are carried without modelling them. A real swap that would fail on the shifted pool is skipped and counted.
- **2026-10-03 · Fee rates are the ones each trade paid.** Every swap event carries its LP, protocol and creator rates, so replays and our fills charge exactly those (our fill uses the latest real swap's rates on that pool, the same market-cap tier to within one trade). Replaying all 353 CORE-2 golden swaps with their own rates reproduces their fees and amounts exactly (test).
- **2026-10-03 · Our order lands after every real transaction of its landing slot** (and after the slot's block row), as a driver hook the engine never sees. Status and balance reports come back as world events after it.
- **2026-10-03 · Landing, failures and expiry.** Each attempt draws its latency and fate from the world seed (two draws, fixed order): lands and executes (p_land, §11 defaults 0.66 PumpSwap, 0.49 curve), lands failed (pays base and priority fee, no tip), or never reaches a block (`dropPpm`, default 0: costs nothing, expires after its last valid block height). A landing that executes still fails if the output after the scenario's extra slippage is below min-out. Rebroadcasts of the same bytes never draw again. Balances are reconciled only once no attempt can still land, so the lifecycle never sees a refused reconcile.
- **2026-10-03 · Scenarios** live in `config/fills.ts` (versioned like the policy). Conservative: p90 latency, slippage shortfall ×1.5, close-based take-profit, no rent recovery. Latency values are placeholders until the dry run measures ours (§21).
- **2026-10-03 · mints.csv is never read by the run.** It holds whole-life facts (graduation time, tape end, censoring). Lifecycle facts reach the engine only as the events of the day they happen; the discovery of a graduation arrives after a per-mint lag drawn from its own seed.
- **2026-10-03 · SOL/USD for sizing and the report** comes from Coinbase Exchange hourly candles (public, no key), fixed series, closed candles only, each usable from its close plus one bar (`packages/backtest/data/sol-usd-1h.csv`, refetchable with `scripts/fetch-sol-usd.ts`). If DATA-1 ships its own off-chain series, the reader takes it (same tag rules).
- **2026-10-03 · Ticks and slot events.** While an intent is in flight, every block delivers a `slot` market event and a lifecycle `tick`; otherwise a heartbeat slot event every 150 blocks (about a minute), so a time stop fires within a minute of its time (pool swaps also wake the strategy). Per-block events while merely holding would add millions of log records over 30 days. Live records its own ticks, which the parity replay feeds back.
- **2026-10-03 · S0 for BT-1** uses universe U2's membership filters (canonical PumpSwap pool, SOL quote, not mayhem) and the policy's time stop; GATE-1's hard rejects and EXIT-1's exits are added to S0 and the strategies in BT-2. The backtest has no open-position cap (§14) but keeps CORE-1's rule of one entry in flight at a time. Fees of entry attempts that never filled are kept as separate costs in the results, not inside a trade.
- **2026-10-03 · Holdout mode returns two things only:** the sealed ledger file's sha256 and, per universe, candidates, entries and entry days (STATS-1's `HoldoutCounts` shape). The file is made read-only; a holdout file that already exists is refused.
- **2026-10-03 · RUN-1 worker contract (§12.4).** RUN-1 defines it because WORKER-1 has not started; WORKER-1 implements the same contract. Health and drills on a loopback HTTP port (no inbound port opens), the feed-drop drill guarded by a per-boot token file; the journal is a JSONL file with a gapless `seq` so completeness is checkable without the ledger schema.
- **2026-10-03 · Restart drills wait for a trade.** Each restart drill opens a window at its pre-set time and kills at the first open trade or unresolved intent in it, or at the window's end (recorded "not mid-trade"). Six are planned so that at least three land mid-trade in a quiet market. Uptime credits each ready sample up to two sample intervals, so any gap (a runner crash, a fallback handover) counts as down.
- **2026-10-03 · Qualifying run starts by a merged file.** The host starts the qualifying run when the deployed release holds `packages/runner/qualifying-run.json`, once per name, and resumes an unfinished run after a reboot. Nobody logs in to the host, and the start is a reviewed change.
- **2026-10-03 · Fallback artifacts.** Each job uploads `dryrun-state` (state, evidence, logs; 90 days) and `dryrun-recorded-<n>` only after the secret scan passed; recorded files move out of the carried state so each job's artifact holds only its own data. Jobs run the worker for 5 h 35 min to leave room for setup, scan and upload inside GitHub's 6 h limit.

## Dry-run simulation (TEST-2, `packages/worker/src/dryrun`)

- **2026-10-03 · The dry run has no send path, proved twice.** Its RPC client has a frozen list of three methods (`getMultipleAccounts`, `getTokenLargestAccounts`, `simulateTransaction`), checked before the scheduler or the network is touched. A test walks the static import graph from `dryrun/index.ts` and fails if it reaches the TX-1 landing client, its adapters or a barrel that re-exports them, names a send method, or loads code dynamically. The builders and the signer policy are imported file by file for that reason, never through `core/src/tx/index.ts`.
- **2026-10-03 · Stand-ins (supervisor ruling): a funded plain wallet for buys, a current holder for sells.** Buy stand-ins are a configured list of public addresses; each must be System-owned with no data and hold the build's worst-case SOL out plus the rent-exempt minimum. Sell stand-ins come from `getTokenLargestAccounts`: the token account must be the owner's ATA, initialized (not frozen), hold at least the position, and its owner must be a plain wallet that can pay the fees. None qualifies: "not simulable", a failure. The record names the stand-in.
- **2026-10-03 · The stand-in build is checked against the bot-wallet build on every trade, not only in tests.** Same programs, same instruction data (amounts, limits, fees, tip), same metas, after replacing the seven wallet-derived accounts (wallet, base ATA, wSOL ATA, both volume accumulators and their wSOL ATAs). Any other difference, including a venue account replaced by a stand-in-derived key, records "structure-mismatch".
- **2026-10-03 · One allowed difference: a sell's close of the base account is left out when the holder has more tokens than the position.** A token account with a balance cannot be closed, and `simulateTransaction` has no state overrides, so keeping the close would fail every such sell for a reason that cannot happen to the bot wallet. The close is removed after building (same compute limit and price as the real shape) and the record says `closeOmitted`; the report counts them. A holder with exactly the position keeps the close.
- **2026-10-03 · Amounts are measured from balances; rent is the rent actually paid (review of PR #28).**
  - **Buys:** the change in the stand-in's base-token balance, against the quote's tokens.
  - **Sells:** the change in the stand-in's lamports, wSOL account and base account, plus the base fee, priority fee and tip the build declares, plus the rent the transaction actually paid. The base-token balance must fall by exactly the position.
  - **Rent paid:** every account the build writes is read before and read back after, and the rent is taken from that state: a new account's lamports (for a wrapped-SOL account, its rent reserve), added bytes × the rent rate for a grown account, and the pump creator vault's top-up to rent-exempt only, since the vault also receives fees. Rent paid above what the build declares is an "amount-check" failure: the builder under-counts. Both figures are recorded.
  - **Where balances come from:** the stand-in's own balances come from the node's `preBalances`, `postBalances` and token balances inside the same simulation, when the provider returns them (Agave 4.3.0 does, checked 2026-10-03). These are atomic, so a transfer landing between our read and the simulation cannot move the amount. Otherwise they come from one `getMultipleAccounts` read at `processed`. The post balances must equal the accounts read back, or the trade is "malformed".
  - **Fee read-back:** the read-back fee payer already has the base and priority fee taken out (docs/evidence/DRYRUN_SMOKE.md).
  - **Why not venue events:** pump's TradeEvent has no field verified to equal the wallet's net SOL on sells. Its buyback fee is non-zero in the golden sells, and that fee's share is unverified.
- **2026-10-03 · The error is |simulated − quoted| / quoted in units of 0.0001 percentage points, rounded up.** The report decides the gate in integers: success share ≥ 95% (every outcome but "simulated" is a failure, "not simulable" and scheduler refusals included), median ≤ 0.5 points and every trade ≤ 2 points, over the successful trades.
- **2026-10-03 · Follow-up: the creator vault's top-up ignores the creator fee paid into it in the same transaction** (review of PR #28). The vault counts as topped up by `rentExempt(0) − its balance before`. If the trade's creator fee lands first and already covers rent, the wallet pays less than that, or nothing, so `rentPaid` can overstate the rent and the measured sell proceeds by up to one rent-exempt minimum (about 0.00065 SOL). This only happens when the vault is missing or below rent. To fix it, take the vault's top-up from the program's order of transfers (or from inner instructions) once that order is verified.
- **2026-10-03 · Dry-run calls run at P2 or P3 on the Helius scheduler, never P0 or P1** (both refused with a RangeError before any request; review of PR #28). The worker uses P2. A scheduler refusal is recorded as a failed trade, never retried silently. Each call is a standard RPC call (1 credit).
- **2026-10-03 · Holders are tried largest first, then by token-account address,** so equal balances never depend on the provider's order. Each record carries `quoteAgeSlots` (read slot − quote slot); the report gives its median.
- **2026-10-03 · A bot-wallet build that the signer policy rejects is not simulated** and counts as a failure ("policy-violation"): live, it would never be signed.
- **2026-10-03 · The live smoke test is a script, opt-in like FEED-1's probe** (`packages/worker/scripts/dryrun-smoke.ts`, `ZEROED_LIVE_PROBE=1` and a Helius key, or `ZEROED_SMOKE_PUBLIC=1` for the keyless public RPC). The worker guard test forbids real network adapters in `test/`, so it is outside the test glob and never runs in CI. Runs are recorded in `docs/evidence/DRYRUN_SMOKE.md`.
