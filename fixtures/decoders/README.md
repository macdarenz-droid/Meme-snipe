# Decoder fixtures

Real Solana mainnet data that the C11 set (`fixtures/mainnet/`) does not hold, captured for the decoder tests of card C03 (A-M02-02, A-M02-03). Public on-chain data only: no keys, no signing, nothing sent.

## Provenance

- Captured: 2026-10-07 05:00:20Z to 05:06:32Z, by a one-off script that sent every request through the M14 gateway of this card (A-M14-01/02): public RPC `https://api.mainnet-beta.solana.com`, research context, priority P4, commitment `confirmed`, one request in flight. The first run used the 50% cap (2/s per method, 5/s in all [LD-26]) and saved nothing; the records come from the second run (0.5/s per method, 1/s in all) and the third (0.25/s and 0.5/s).
- The public RPC still answered 429 with `Retry-After: 10` sixteen times over five runs (about 100 requests): its per-IP limits are shared with every user of this session's egress IP. The gateway honoured every `Retry-After`. The first run stopped at its first 429. In the second, seven 429s came within 2.5 minutes, alternating with successes, so a count of consecutive answers never reached three; that run is why the gateway now stops a provider at the third 429 or 403 within ten minutes (A-M14-02). The third run, under that rule, had two. A fourth run (a just-created mint) and a fifth (more trades with a top-level pump instruction) each met three 429s within ten minutes; the gateway stopped the provider, as the owner rule requires, both saved nothing, and no further run was made.
- Review fixes (C03-R1-1, C03-R1-5): two more runs on 2026-10-07 05:45:18Z to 05:50:55Z through the same gateway at 0.2/s per method and 0.25/s in all (45 requests, a hard budget per run, every answer cached so none was repeated). No 429 or 403 came back. They added the just-created mint and two trades below, and looked for a lagged completion under a top-level pump instruction (see Limits).
- Review round 2 (C03-R1-1, option (b)): one more run on 2026-10-07 05:59:12Z to 06:01:27Z through the same gateway at the same rates (26 requests of a 45-request budget, a fresh cache). No 429 or 403 came back. It looked only for a lagged completion under a top-level pump instruction, found none, and recorded nothing (see Limits).
- Transactions by `getTransaction {encoding: json, maxSupportedTransactionVersion: 1}`; accounts by `getMultipleAccounts {encoding: base64}`. Each record keeps the full RPC result, its slot, version, `found_via` and `reason`. `fetched_at` is the time the record was written, within seconds of the request.
- Program IDs [EX-01]; the bonding-curve PDA `["bonding-curve", mint]` was derived with `tools/fixtures/lib.ts` (C11). Events were recognised by the event-CPI prefix and the discriminators of the pinned IDLs (commit `cb188ce08b5069196eef1f3e4a0c43b70099793b`).

## Limits

- Ten of the twelve recorded pump `TradeEvent`s sit under a top-level pump instruction; the other two (the version-1 transaction and the lagged completion) are router-routed. Under the supervisor ruling on A-M02-03 logic 3 (2026-10-07: the direct invoker, found by `stackHeight`, must be the emitting program) all twelve decode.
- No lagged completion under a top-level pump instruction was found. Of the 25 newest signatures of the withdraw authority (2026-10-07, slots 454129208-454131688), 12 were migrations: 8 had a transaction on their curve in the same slot just before them (lag 0), and 4 lagged by 1-3 slots (`2hzLJ1w1`, `424S1kgF`, `3YjPBALe`, `457YBhKu`). The completing buys of three of these were found and all are router-routed (top-level programs other than pump); for `424S1kgF` the three newest earlier transactions on its curve carried no pump event. With the recorded 3-slot case, every lagged completion seen (4 of 4) is router-routed, which the first self-CPI rule of A-M02-03 (top-level parent) refused. The search stopped there (owner rule: minimum sample). Round 2 read the 25 newest withdraw-authority signatures again (slots 454133488-454135666), skipping the round-1 slots: 6 were new migrations. 2 had lag 0 (`3qSYm2wT`, `4TYQbT3D`), 3 lagged by 3, 2 and 5 slots (`41sfh2HF`, `5DZEqoGU`, `4Q8eRXGk`), and for `zM4qzEDS` the five newest earlier transactions on its curve had all failed, so its lag is unknown. All three lagged completions are router-routed again: top-level Jupiter v6 (`JUP6LkbZ…`, `4ksM6g44`), `FLASHX8D…` (`p263dfqo`) and `7eND9Vgs…` (`3vKLJtfG`). So 7 of 7 lagged completions seen are router-routed, while 10 of 12 recorded trades sit under a top-level pump instruction. The supervisor's ruling on A-M02-03 logic 3 (review finding C03-R1-1) resolved the lagged case: pump invokes its own event-CPI inside the router's call, so the recorded lagged completion gives `pump_complete`.
- No mayhem-mode trade, no short legacy `BondingCurve` account (the shortest curve seen is 141 bytes, longer than the 125 bytes the pinned IDL describes), no frozen token account and no non-canonical pool were found in this sample. The tests derive those cases from the real records above and say so; they are open items on the board (`docs/TASKS.md`).
