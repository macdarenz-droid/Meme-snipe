## N1-audience-gains: testable-with-paid-data

IDEA 1 (audience gains, meaning a meme's recent buyers realise SOL gains in other coins, then buy more of that meme within 6 h). Verdict: stage 1 cannot be run now. The remaining 3.8M Helius credits pay only for a pilot that is too small to give a result. A full test needs about 56 consecutive chain days of per-wallet swap data. There are two ways to get it. (a) Free: reuse the core Old Faithful archive days. As of 2026-10-07 none are published (GitHub releases list only `preview`), and whether the archive will serve the scanner is uncertain. (b) Helius full blocks: about 15M credits, which is about 11M (roughly US$56) beyond the allocation and needs the owner's approval. This is why the verdict is 'paid data'. If the archive serves, the paid part goes away.

Evidence check: the Sun (2023) paper is real (opened). It only weakly supports this mechanism. It tracks about 1M Ethereum wallets from 2021 to 2022. Identification is the random rarity of NFT mints, with fixed effects for collection, month and number of items minted. Getting a rare NFT raised the next month's log volume of purchases of 'lottery-like' cryptos by 0.0047 (about 0.5%, p<0.05). Total crypto trading did not rise (0.0066, not significant). Most importantly, the effect was absent for minters who SOLD, i.e. realised the gain (−0.0022, not significant), and present for holders (0.0061, p<0.01). The author rejects house money as the main driver. So the reviewer's mechanism is not what the paper found. Realised gains in coin C turning into buying of a different coin B within 6 h is a different claim, at a different horizon, with a different kind of shock (earned, not random). The prior is low.

Data findings:
1. Buying full wallet histories is unaffordable. Sample measured: 20 buyer wallets of two live memes. Median 106 signatures a day; median 58 successful transactions a day; 2 of the 20 are bots at 13k and 235k a day. At 10 credits per 100 transactions, one meme's 7-day buyer cohort costs about 1.8M to 5M credits. Market-wide it costs hundreds of millions.
2. The cheapest Helius route is getBlock over full blocks. The repo measured about 250k credits a chain day (plus about 18k margin), at 1 credit per call as the Helius docs state.
3. The repo's own pump.fun + PumpSwap scanner keeps every curve trade and every canonical-pool trade, with the owner of each trade. That supports a pump-only version of the test. It misses other venues, so a coin a wallet bought elsewhere shows up as an unknown-cost sell.
4. Better: the scanners already read full blocks. A venue-neutral table of each owner's token and SOL balance changes could be added at zero extra credits. It would cover every DEX and show transfers directly. It has to be added before those days are read, because the owner's never-download-twice rule (ARCHIVE-NODUP) means each day is read only once. It also changes a scanner the core project owns.
5. Even if stage 1 passes, running it live needs the whole pump trade stream. The repo measured that at about 14M Helius credits a month, more than the plan's 10M.

Recommended order: pre-register first. Then a 1,000–2,000-wallet coverage check with getTransactionsForAddress, about 0.1–0.9M credits. Then stage 1 on the archive days only if they arrive. I would not spend the 3.8M on a 14-day block pull: after warm-up it leaves 6–7 test days, too few to judge.

Discrepancy to fix: the Helius credits page (fetched 2026-10-07) says archival calls cost 1 credit. The repo's research ledger (research/execution-audit/heli.py) counts 10 per call 'conservatively'. Past spends may therefore be overstated about 10 times; the owner's Helius dashboard is the authority.

## N2-incentive-expiry: not-testable

Idea 2 (short a meme perp after a documented temporary holding incentive ends) fails its own kill condition: there are too few events to short. The only Solana meme with official, published holding-contest terms and a tradable perp is TRUMP. Its organiser has run a chain of time-weighted leaderboard contests: E1 dinner, window 23 Apr to 12 May 2025; E2 Mar-a-Lago luncheon, 12 Mar to 10 Apr 2026; E3 World Cup suite, 12 May to 1 Jul 2026 on the official terms (news says 8 May to 5 Jul); E4 F1 Singapore, 18 Jul to 1 Oct 2026; E5 Gala Dinner, 30 Sep or 5 Oct to 12 Nov 2026 at 1 PM ET (the official pages disagree on the start); E6 Super Bowl, 12 Nov 2026 to 7 Feb 2027. Only E1 to E3 fall before the holdout wall (2026-09-21 14:00Z). Of those, only E1 and E2 are clearly clean expiries. Whether E3 was clean depends on when F1 was announced, which is unverified. From E4 on, each contest rolls into the next: the next window opens at or before the snapshot, and a 10% score carryover goes only to holders who keep their balance. So the 'expiry' is not an expiry. The E5 page also requires VIPs to keep their snapshot balance until the 22 Nov event. No other Solana meme qualified. Searches of BONK, WIF, POPCAT, FARTCOIN, PENGU, MELANIA and ai16z found only reward-token distributions, migrations and claims, which the reviewer said to keep separate. The total is 2 to 3 clean pre-wall events, at most 6 by Feb 2027, all on one coin. With TRUMP's 24-hour volatility (computed only on pre-wall days outside ±10 days of the cutoffs; standard deviation 5.75%, robust 3.25%), 3 events can only detect a 24-hour abnormal move of about 5 to 9%. The pre-funding gate's 300 out-of-sample trades can never be reached. Liebi is verified but is only indirect evidence. 'Raining Cryptos' (St. Gallen, version dated 14 Dec 2021, SSRN 3918432) covers 67 events (51 hard forks and 16 airdrops) on 19 parent coins, 2014 to 2020. It found no announcement effect and no significant run-up before the snapshot (CAR over days −10 to −1: −0.8%, not significant). On the snapshot day the AAR was −4.65% (t = −3.19), and the CAR over days 0 to 5 was −12.29% (t = −3.37). The drop scales with the value of the distributed coin: −6.70% when the new coin was later listed, −2.77% when it never was. That is an ex-entitlement drop on a value distribution, the very class the reviewer excludes. It is not holding demand unwinding after an off-chain privilege. For mechanism, press reports exist but no study: Arkham (2 May 2025) shows time-weighted scoring let a top-25 holder sell before the cutoff and keep his place, and Decrypt (23 May 2025) reports that 17 of the top 25 VIP wallets moved tokens to exchanges within days of the 12 May snapshot. What the bot could do: a 1x short on Hyperliquid's TRUMP perp. It has traded since 2025-01-18, max leverage is 10x today, and taker fee is 0.045% a side. Pre-wall 24-hour funding was small: median |24h sum| 0.033%, 90th percentile 0.14%. That is 2 to 4 trades a year, behind several blockers: derivatives and cross-chain use are an open owner decision (edge.md line 180), Australian legality is unconfirmed (edge.md §10.5), the trade needs a separate Hyperliquid key with USDC margin, and SOL accounting needs a SOL hedge or conversion. Recommendation: drop it as a strategy. If wanted, log E5 and E6 descriptively at no cost; no trading rule can come from them.

## N3-buybacks: testable-with-helius

Idea 3 can be tested on Solana, but only through one usable source, and the odds are low.

The reviewer's examples do not fit:
- FLOKI's claim is true (docs.floki.com: "A 1% fee ... 50% of those fees allocated to buying and burning $FLOKI"), but FLOKI is a BNB/Ethereum token, so it is out of scope.
- Keyrock's report ("Designing Token Buybacks", Amir Hajian, about Oct 2025) covers 12 DeFi protocols and no memecoins. Its only price test is a timing rule on one protocol: the moving-average trigger "only predicted stronger forward returns 39% of the time" over 550 buyback days. It is not evidence that buyback demand predicts returns.
- BONK and PUMP are one token each, so there is no cross-section.
  - Both programmes are funded by meme-market activity (BONKbot fees, pump.fun revenue), so the buyback series moves with the "market boom" control.
  - BONKbot buys are small: about $16.6k a day in Q2 2026 (Blockworks Q2 2026: $1.51M revenue, 100% of fees routed to BONK buys).
  - BONK.fun's 51% fee redirect to the Bonk Inc. (BNKK) treasury is not shown as market buying: Blockworks says the treasury "held flat at 2.48T" all of Q2.
  - PUMP is pump.fun's platform token, not a meme.
- BOOST is excluded: it spends held-back migration liquidity over about 5 minutes, it is not revenue, and its window was already tested.

**The usable source: pump.fun "Tokenized Agents"**
- What it is: a toggle that sends a coin's revenue into hourly buy-and-burn of that same coin.
  - It was launched on 2026-03-13. New launches with it were stopped on 2026-06-30; existing coins keep running.
  - pump.fun's terms page says "Token buy backs occur on an hourly basis" and that bought tokens are burned.

  - 30,394 coins had at least one SOL buyback. 4,660 bought back at least 1 SOL, 786 at least 10 SOL, 139 at least 100 SOL, and 4 at least 1,000 SOL.
  - Lifetime buybacks total 75,746 SOL plus about $39k in USDC (103 coins).
- Every buyback is a real market purchase, measurable to the lamport. The event `AgentBuybackTriggerEvent` records the amount spent, the tokens burned, the venue and a timestamp.
  - All buybacks are signed by one buyback authority (`GmFrDZT2…`).
  - That wallet has 174,481 transactions from 2026-03-12 to 2026-10-07 (UTC). By month: Mar 19.8k, Apr 22.1k, May 47.9k, Jun 63.1k, Jul 6.7k, Aug 13.4k, Sep 1.2k, Oct 1–7 0.25k.

  - coins with at least 10 SOL lifetime: about 12k token-days (median 8 active days over a 92-day span);
  - coins with 1–10 SOL: about 8k token-days (median 2 active days).

**The catch**
- The "revenue" is mostly the coin's own creator fees, not outside income.
  - Invoice payments total only 781 SOL, against 75,746 SOL of buybacks.
  - The sampled funding transactions are creator-fee payouts (DistributeCreatorFees) straight into the agent accounts.
- So daily buyback spend is roughly creator-fee rate × the coin's own past volume × the buyback share. The creator fee is 0.05–0.95% on PumpSwap (venues.md).
  - The buyback signal therefore nearly duplicates the coin's own volume.
  - At most it is under 1% of the coin's own volume, against a round-trip cost of 1–2.5% (edge.md §10.1).
- The flow is now almost gone: about 35–40 authority transactions a day in Sep–Oct 2026, versus about 2,000 in June. Even a positive Stage 1 would leave very few live trades unless pump.fun reopens the feature.

## N4-failed-tx: testable-with-helius

The paper is real and the replication repo is real, but neither looks at hidden demand. The paper is Zheng, Wan, Lo, Xie, Yang, "Why Does My Transaction Fail? A First Look at Failed Transactions on the Solana Blockchain", PACMSE vol. 2 (ISSTA) pp. 1489-1512, 2025, DOI 10.1145/3728943, arXiv 2504.18055. It is a software-engineering study: 2.90B non-vote transactions, 52% of them failed, on 53 sampled days from Aug 2023 to Jul 2024. It sorts error messages by hand into 10 types (price or profit not met 47.99%, invalid status 19.19%, validity expiration 17.72%) and blames each failure on the outermost program. It says nothing about pump.fun and nothing about order flow or prices. The repo (github.com/ZXXYy/Solana_Failed_Tx, head e69ce66, 2025-04-15) ships only sampled data (the full set is about 5 TB). Its failed_txs_one_year.log shows 3.46M failures with pump as the outermost program. Of those, 962,289 are buy-slippage (6002 TooMuchSolRequired) and 206,838 are sell-slippage (6003), a 4.65:1 ratio. Insufficient funds (1.23M) and panics (0.72M) are bigger than either.

Our data paths:
- **rpcscan (Helius getBlock, full, base64):** captures failed transactions with err, logs, inner instructions and signer. scan.go:782-806 writes a failed row and a raw record, but only for hash-sampled mints (-sample 0.05 in CI).
- **copytrading/collect.mjs:** drops failed transactions (line 23). Its logsSubscribe feed has no signer anyway.

What the 3 real getBlock blocks in repo testdata show (2026-10-01 11:40 UTC):
- 277 transactions mention pump or PumpSwap and 124 of them failed (45%). But 112 of those failed inside bot programs before calling pump at all.
- Of the 154 transactions that actually call pump or PumpSwap, 12 failed (7.8%). Only 5 of these are slippage failures (about 3.5% of successful ones): pump 6002 x1, AMM 6040 x3 (2 wallets), AMM 6004 from sell.rs x1.
- In the same blocks, 12 failures carry Custom 6002 from other programs (cpamdpZC... x7, dbcij3LW... x5), so the error code alone misleads. The program that failed and the Anchor source path in the logs must decide direction.

The signal is cheap to build but has low odds. Failed slippage attempts are mostly bots racing a price jump, so they are likely redundant with the recent price move, which is exactly the reviewer's kill condition.

## N5-N6-community-semantic: testable-with-helius

IDEA 5 (community crossing). Both Weng, Menczer and Ahn papers are real, and I opened both.
- 2013 (Sci Rep 3:2522): a 10% Twitter sample from 24 Mar to 25 Apr 2012, communities found with Infomap on a reciprocal-follow graph of 595,460 users, features from each new hashtag's first 50 tweets, random forest. For the top 10% most viral hashtags by users, precision was about 7 times random and more than 3 times a model without community features.
- 2014 (ICWSM 8:535-544): 400,020 nodes, InfoMap and LinkClustering, first 25/50/100 tweets, 13 features, 10-fold cross-validation (not split by time). Community features predicted best, and early popularity predicted weakly.
- What they predicted was hashtag popularity, not price. Their communities came from an exposure network (who follows whom), measured over the same period, not before it.
- A wallet co-purchase community measures similar behaviour, not exposure. So the evidence carries over only by analogy.
- The design below is feasible. It needs full-day, wallet-level trade data (every bonding-curve trade and every canonical PumpSwap trade, with the owner wallet). Our scanner already keeps exactly that.
- Source: no historical day is published yet. Data comes from the free Old Faithful archive if it serves us again, or from Helius getBlock: about 250k credits a day as measured by our pilot, 1 credit per call per Helius docs. Ten to 14 days cost about 2.5-3.5M of the 3.8M unallocated credits.
- The same days would also serve idea 1 (audience gains) and idea 4 (failed transactions; the scanner's sample rate must be raised first, because failed trades are kept only for 5% of mints today) and price idea 6. That shared use is the only way the spend makes sense.

## dataset-architect: testable-with-helius

The shared dataset can be built with Helius. Today's tools need one change first: failed transactions are kept for only 5% of coins, and some wallet columns are missing. Only 9 days of chain fit the ~3.5M credit budget, so the run must be split. Phase 1 is a 9-day discovery window before the wall. Phase 2 is an untouched 14-day holdout from 2026-10-22, paid from the next credit cycle.

KEY CORRECTION ON CREDITS. The Helius credits page (fetched 2026-10-07) says getBlock, getBlocks, getTransaction and getSignaturesForAddress cost 1 credit each. Only getTransactionsForAddress and getTransfersByAddress cost 10. The probes' heli.py counts 10 per call 'conservatively' (research/execution-audit/heli.py:18). So the '10 credits per archival call' convention, and the '3.8M unallocated' derived from it, may overstate real spend about 10 times. Only the owner's dashboard can settle this. At 10 per getBlock, 3.5M buys less than one chain day, and the Helius part of this plan is not viable.

KEY CORRECTION ON THE WINDOW. The research holdout wall is 2026-09-21T14:00Z (edge.md:111; launch-probe PREREG:32 'Nothing at or after 2026-09-21T14:00Z is read'). Post-wall days 10-03 to 10-11 must not be used for discovery. Days 09-12 to 09-21 are U1-B's holdout (HANDOVER:593).

WHAT RPCSCAN EXTRACTS TODAY. It reads blocks with Helius getBlock, re-encodes each one as an archive node, and runs the scanner's unchanged decoders (most rpcscan files are symlinks to ../scanner). It writes units of 4,500 slots. A unit is about 20 minutes of chain at October slot times; the code comment still says ~30 minutes.
- Kept: every bonding-curve trade and every canonical PumpSwap trade of every coin. Rows carry slot, tx_idx, signature, user, signer and user_token_owner (the real owner). They also carry SOL and token amounts, reserves (curve after the trade, PumpSwap before), every fee, jito_tip and ix_name.
- Also kept: token movements of every coin whose address ends in 'pump', delegations, all creates and migrations, the hourly census, and per-block counts including n_pump_failed.
- Failed transactions are kept as rows (slot, tx_idx, signature, signer, fee, CU, programs, mint_hint, error as bincode hex) only for the 5% hash sample (scanner/scan.go:804). These rows carry no side and no slippage limit.
- Not kept: the owner's token balance before and after a trade, the signer's SOL balance, SOL transfers, non-canonical pools of 95% of coins, and swaps on other venues (counted only, for sampled coins).
- rpcscan's digest.go is a pilot fingerprint for checking units against each other, not a research table.

PER IDEA (no returns computed):
1. Audience gains: testable with Helius, stage 1 only, on the shared dataset. Cohorts and realised SOL gains come from pump and PumpSwap trades. Tokens bought before the window or received by transfer have unknown cost and are flagged. The prior-7-day cohort uses up 7 of the 9 discovery days, leaving 2 decision days, which is enough for feasibility counts only. A powered test needs the 14-day holdout. Sun's paper is real (AFA listing).
2. Incentive expiry: not testable at useful power. Hyperliquid has the data: 234 perps (56 delisted, which must stay in), and TRUMP has hourly funding back to 2025-04-23 (checked). But the only well-documented hold campaign found is the TRUMP dinner (time-weighted holdings, 23 Apr to 12 May 2025, top 220). Expected events are judged under 10, not counted. Liebi's paper (St. Gallen 2021) is about distributions, which the reviewer says to keep separate.
3. Buybacks: not testable at useful power. Our data shows only pump.fun's own flows (BOOST buy-and-burn, the buyback_fee field), not a coin's own revenue-funded buying. FLOKI is outside pump and PumpSwap (its docs were not opened). Keyrock (via The Defiant) found no clear evidence that the market rewards buybacks.
4. Failed transactions: testable with Helius after the change. It needs failed rows for every coin with side, amount, limit and a decoded error. Slippage codes from the IDLs: pump 6002, 6003 and 6042; PumpSwap 6004 and 6040. Failed Jito bundles never reach the chain, so the visible failures leave out bundle and revert-protected senders. The cited paper is real (arXiv 2504.18055, repo ZXYy/Solana_Failed_Tx), but its data covers Aug 2023 to Jul 2024, before PumpSwap, so only its method carries over.
5. Community crossing: testable with Helius on the same dataset. Funding clusters need a new table of SOL transfers and can only use transfers inside the window. It has the same 7-day lookback problem as idea 1. Weng, Menczer and Ahn are real (Scientific Reports 2013, ICWSM 2014); their outcome was hashtag popularity, not prices.
6. SOLMEMES: the paper is unverified (not found in two searches). Name, symbol and URI of every create are on chain (~51k a day). Descriptions sit off-chain behind the URI, and their availability is biased toward survivors. MemeTrans (arXiv 2602.13480) covers Raydium-era coins (Dec 2024 to Mar 2025), so it cannot serve today's PumpSwap market.
